# A first budget on little money

## When to use this

Use this when a project has between nothing and a few hundred dollars and needs a budget, and each time an agent or the founder suggests paying for a tool, an ad, materials or a service. Until the project has regular sales, this small budget is the money picture the founder sees. The larger models can wait.

## Before you start

Read from the records:

1. The starting money: what the founder has set aside for the project, in the project's currency. If the records do not say, build the budget on zero and say in the hand-back that the figure is missing.
2. What already goes out: every subscription, plan or tool the project pays for, with its renewal date. Count what the team's own work costs if the founder is on a paid plan.
3. What has come in. Real payments only.
4. The stage: no page yet, page live, first followers, first sale.
5. What the project already has at no cost. Its public page is hosted free, and it has a free address to send email from. Those two cover a first week.

Two terms to explain once to the founder. A budget is a plan for what may go out each month. Runway is how many months the money lasts at the current pace.

## Steps

1. **Start every line at zero.** For each thing the project wants, find the free way first and write it down: the free page and sending address, the free tier of a tool, the founder's own phone for photos and video, selling by hand before building a store. A paid option goes in only when the free one has hit a real limit, and the line says which limit.
2. **Order what is worth paying for by what it makes possible.** Amounts are Estimates as of 2026. Check the current price before showing one.

   | When | Worth paying for | Why then | Rough cost |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | Before anyone has said yes | Nothing, as a rule | Nothing has been tested yet | $0 |
   | Someone has said yes to an offer | What that one sale takes to deliver: materials, a room, postage | The sale itself covers it | Less than the sale brings in |
   | The first payment is being set up | The payment tool's fee | It is charged per sale, usually with no monthly charge | About 3% plus a small fixed fee per card payment |
   | Marketing email starts | A postal address other than the founder's home, if they want one | US marketing email has to show a postal address | $5 to $30 a month for a post office box |
   | After the first sale | A domain name | The address becomes the founder's own | $10 to $20 a year |
   | A free tier runs out | That tool's lowest plan, billed monthly | The limit now costs real work | Varies |
   | After several sales | A small paid test to reach more people, with its own cap | There is an offer known to sell | The founder sets it |

   Registration, licenses and tax belong to the Legal lead. Ask before putting a figure on them.
3. **Propose two limits for the founder to decide.** A monthly limit: with starting money, propose the amount that makes it last at least six months (starting money divided by six), or less when less is needed. A single-item limit: propose that anything above 10% of the starting money, and any new monthly charge of any size, is its own decision. With no starting money both limits are zero and costs come out of sales, the way a preorder funds its own batch.
4. **Prefer costs that end.** One-off before monthly. Monthly before yearly until a tool has been in use for three months, because a yearly discount ties up money the project may need. A fee per sale before a fixed fee. Record every renewal date, and every free trial that took a card with the day it starts charging.
5. **Keep project money on its own list.** One list of what went out and what came in for the project, apart from the founder's personal spending, from the first dollar. Whether to open a separate bank account is the founder's call. Suggest it when the first sale arrives.
6. **Label every number.** A real payment or charge cites its record. A price you looked up is an Estimate with the date you checked it. Anything about a future month is a Forecast. With no bank connection, say the summary is built from what the founder entered.
7. **Show it in three lines.**
   - You have: the money set aside, plus what has come in, minus what has gone out.
   - Going out this month: the total so far and planned, against the monthly limit.
   - It lasts: the runway in months at this pace, or "no monthly costs" when there are none.

   A fourth line appears only when there is something to decide: the next thing worth paying for, its cost, and what has to be true first.
8. **Answer three questions for every proposed cost.** What real outcome does it make possible this month? What is the free way, and where does it fall short? How is it canceled? If the first answer is "none yet", recommend waiting.

## What good looks like

Maya has set aside $300 for her sewing classes. Nothing is billed monthly. Two people have paid $25 each. She bought fabric for $22.50, and the card fees on the two payments came to $2.06.

```
You have: $325.44 ($300 set aside, plus $50.00 from two bookings, minus $24.56 spent).
Going out this month: $24.56 of your $50 limit (fabric $22.50, card fees $2.06).
It lasts: nothing is billed monthly, so the money is not running down.
Next worth paying for: a room for the second class, about $30 (Estimate), once four of the six places are booked.
```

The same week an agent proposes a booking tool at $29 a month. The three answers: it makes nothing new possible this month, because six bookings are easy to take by reply; the free way is a reply and a calendar, and it falls short at around 15 bookings a month (Estimate); it cancels online at any time. The recommendation is to wait, and the fourth line stays as it is.

A project with no starting money reads: "You have: $0. Going out this month: $0 of your $0 limit. It lasts: nothing is billed monthly. Next worth paying for: materials for the first order, paid from that order."

## Output

```
Starting money: <amount, or "not in the records: built on zero">
Free ways in use: <list>
Paid lines: <item, amount, monthly or one-off, what it makes possible, how to cancel, label>
Limits proposed for the founder: monthly <amount>, single item <amount>
Three lines: You have / Going out this month / It lasts
Next worth paying for: <item, cost, what has to be true first>
Dates to watch: <renewals and trial ends>
Sources: <the record, or the date checked, behind each number>
```

## Check before you hand it over

- Every paid line names the free way it replaces and the limit that was hit.
- The total for the month is at or under the limit the founder set. Nothing in the budget assumes a higher limit.
- Every number is a record, an Estimate with a date, or a Forecast. None is bare.
- The three lines add up. Redo the sum once from the records.
- The summary is in the project's currency, and a first-timer would know every word in it.
- Every renewal and trial end has a date.
- You paid for nothing, ordered nothing and subscribed to nothing.

## What needs the founder

Every payment, purchase and subscription, and every cancellation. The two limits and any change to them. Opening any account. You compare, recommend and prepare. The founder pays.

## If a tool is missing

With no bank or billing connection, build the budget from what the founder has entered and label it that way. With no way to look up a current price, show the last figure you have as an Estimate marked "not verified" and say what to check.
