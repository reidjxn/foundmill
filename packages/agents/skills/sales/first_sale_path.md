# The path to a first sale

## When to use this

Use this when a project has a public page and no payment yet, and the question is how to get from one to the other. Use it too when someone proposes building more before anyone has paid.

The numbers behind a price belong to the skill for what one sale is worth, and the words of the page to the page copy skill. This skill is the route: the test, the ask, the follow-up and what counts as a signal.

## Before you start

Read from the records:

1. The kind of project: a service, a simple physical product, or software or content.
2. What exists today that a person could receive this week, even if it is delivered by hand.
3. Who has shown interest: followers of the page, people who replied, people the founder named. Note the record for each.
4. What the project can do: take a payment or a booking, send email from the project's address, change the page.
5. Whether the founder has started distribution. Until they have, the founder does the asking from their own accounts and the team prepares.
6. Any price the founder has mentioned, and their starting money.

If a fact is missing, state your assumption in the hand-back and continue.

## Steps

1. **Choose the cheapest test that asks for money.** The test is an offer a real person can say yes to this week by paying or booking. Compliments cost nothing to give, so they prove nothing. Pick the offer by kind:

   | Kind | The offer | How a yes is taken | What must be deliverable |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | Service | One named session or piece of work, with a date and a price | A booking for a set time, with a payment link in the confirmation | The session, done by the founder |
   | Simple product | A preorder with a delivery date, or a small batch of 10 to 20 | Payment at order, online or in person | The batch, by the stated date |
   | Software or content | A paid first version that does one thing, or a founding-customer price | Payment up front for access | That one thing, even if part of it is done by hand at first |

   A founding-customer price is a lower price for the first few buyers in return for their feedback. The offer says plainly what exists today and what does not yet.
2. **Put the whole ask in one short message.** What it is, who it is for, the price, how to say yes, and a date only when the date is real (the class is on the 21st, the batch is ordered on Friday). For example: "I am running a two-hour sewing class for beginners on Saturday, Nov 21. Six places, $25 each. Want one? Reply and I will send you the booking link." Never invent a deadline or a shortage to hurry someone.
3. **Ask the warmest people first, one at a time.** People who replied come first, then followers of the page, then people the founder named. Ten personal asks usually teach more than one post seen by a thousand. Until the founder has started distribution, each ask is a draft the founder sends from their own account. After that, the team may write to followers who agreed to hear from the project, with the founder's approval and inside the daily cap.
4. **Follow up twice, then stop.** Once after two or three days, with something new: an answer to their question, a photo, the date. Once more a week later. After that, leave the person alone unless they write. A "no" is useful. Reply with one question, "What would have made this a yes?", and record the answer in their words.
5. **Grade what comes back.**

   | What the person did | Signal |
   | --- | --- |
   | Paid | A sale. Record it as a win |
   | Paid a deposit or preordered | Strong |
   | Booked a time, or introduced another buyer | Real |
   | Asked the price, the date or "how do I get it" | Interest. Answer, then ask again |
   | Gave an email address to hear more | Weak. Worth one follow-up |
   | Said "love it" or "great idea", or liked a post | None. Do not count it |

   The test for a signal is whether the person gave something up: money, time on their calendar, or their name in front of a friend.
6. **Read the result after ten asks.** As a rule of thumb (ten is a small number, so label any rate an Estimate): with no payment and no booking from ten warm asks, change one thing, either who it is for, the offer or the price, and ask ten more. With one or more, deliver, then ask ten more people with the same words. Change one thing at a time so you can tell what made the difference.
7. **Prepare everything around the payment.** Draft two or three price options, each with what is left after costs, and recommend one. Draft the page section with the offer and its button, the confirmation message and the thank-you, and say what the buyer gets and when. A preorder states its delivery date and what happens if the date slips: the buyer is told and offered their money back. In the US a seller has to ship by the promised date, or within 30 days when no date was given, or offer a refund (as of 2026, check the current rule). Ask the Legal lead for the short terms a sale needs.
8. **After the first payment, deliver before anything else.** Then thank the buyer, ask one question about why they bought, and ask whether their words may be quoted. Record the sale as a win with the payment record.

## What good looks like

Maya's sewing classes are a service. The page has six followers.

The offer is one two-hour class with six places on Saturday, Nov 21, at $25. Maya chose $25 from three prepared options ($15, $25, $35) and set up the payment link herself. The team drafted the ask, and Maya sent it to the six followers and four friends, one by one.

After three days: two people booked and paid, one asked "is it for total beginners?", four said it sounds great, three were silent. Graded, that is two sales, one interest and seven with no signal. The team drafts an answer for the person who asked, and a short follow-up with a photo of the room for the three who were silent. The four compliments are left out of the count.

The hand-back says: "2 of 10 paid (payment records attached). Keep the offer and the price. Next: ten more asks with the same words."

## Output

```
Kind: service, product, or software or content
Offer: <what, for whom, price options, date if real>
How a yes is taken: <booking, preorder or payment link; who sets it up>
Ask: <the message, ready to use>
Who is asked first: <names or records, warmest first; who sends>
Follow-ups: <two, with dates>
Results so far: <asked, paid, booked, interest, none; the record for each>
Next step: <deliver, repeat, or the one thing to change>
Assumptions and gaps: <anything the records did not hold>
```

## Check before you hand it over

- The offer can be delivered this week, or by a stated date that is real.
- The ask names a price. An ask with no price tests nothing.
- No deadline, shortage, customer or quote is invented.
- Compliments are not counted as signals.
- Every message to a person outside is either sent by the founder or approved by the founder.
- The price is shown as options for the founder, with one recommended.
- Each result cites its record. A simulated payment is not a sale.

## What needs the founder

The price. Opening and connecting the account that takes payment, and agreeing to that account's rules. Turning on any payment link or booking page. Any refund. Every message from their own accounts, and approval of any message the team sends. Prepare each so it is one read and one action.

## If a tool is missing

With no payment tool, a yes is a booking by reply, and the founder collects payment in a way they already can, such as in person. Say plainly that no payment has been recorded. With no sending tool, hand the founder the drafts. With no way to change the page, hand over the new section as text and say it is not live.
