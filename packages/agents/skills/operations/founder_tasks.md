# A task only the founder can do

## When to use this

Use this once a day, when you pick the single thing the founder is offered to do, and whenever a plan reaches a step no agent can take: a message from the founder's own phone, a conversation, a face on camera, a price.

Decisions that arrive as notes follow the notes skill. This skill is for the task the founder does with their own hands.

## Before you start

Read from the records:

1. This week's real outcome and how far along it is.
2. What the founder has done so far and what they reported back. A task builds on the last one they finished.
3. Any decision already open for them. If one is open, answering it is today's task, and you add nothing else.
4. The founder's first name, their weekly hours and how they prefer to reach people, if the profile says.
5. What the team has ready that a task could use: the live page address, a draft, a list of names.

Know what a founder task is. It is something only a person can do: reach people who know them from their own accounts, talk with someone, appear in a photo or a video, decide something about the business, or open an account that has to be in their name. Work that belongs to the team or the platform is never a founder task. Setting up agents, checking an agent's work, finding the team's documents and reviewing settings stay with the team.

## Steps

1. **Pick the task that moves this week's outcome most.** Common ones:

   | When | Task | What it gives the plan |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | The page just went live | Send the page to five people who might want it | First reactions, in real words |
   | Someone replied | Have one conversation, 10 to 15 minutes, by call or by message | What they do today and what they would pay for |
   | Before the first post | Record a 30 to 60 second video on a phone, or take three photos | Something real for the page and the post |
   | Before taking payment | Decide the price from two or three prepared options | The number the page and the payment link need |
   | After the first sale | Ask the customer one question, and whether you may quote them | A true sentence for the page |
   | Any week | Post once in a group or place they already belong to | Reach the team cannot get on its own |

2. **Size it for 15 to 30 minutes.** If it is bigger, offer its first 20 minutes. "Talk to ten customers" becomes "message one person who replied and ask the two questions below".
3. **Give one reason, in terms of their project.** One sentence on what they will know or have afterward: "Five real reactions will show which words on your page people repeat back."
4. **Write the how-to in three steps or fewer.** Each step is one action a person can do on a phone.
5. **Attach a ready draft where one helps.** A message to copy, two questions to ask, a four-line outline for a video. Write it in the founder's vocabulary and say only what the records support. Suggest they change the first line so it sounds like them. The team does not send from the founder's personal accounts.
6. **Say how to report back, and ask for little.** One line is enough: a number and what someone said. Offer three replies: "Done", "Did some", "Not today". Each is a fine answer.
7. **Use what comes back.** Record who was contacted and every reply in the person's exact words. The words go into the page copy, the names into follow-ups, the price onto the page. In tomorrow's note, tell the founder what their task changed. A task whose result disappears will not get done a second time.
8. **When a task is not done, change the task.** Offer a smaller version once, or a different task that serves the same outcome. A founder who would rather not call someone can send a message. After two days with no reply, pick another task. Do not mention the days.
9. **Write as someone on their side.** Mark a first when it is one: "This is your first message to a stranger about it." Never write "overdue", "still", "reminder", a count of missed tasks, or a streak. Nothing in a task suggests the founder is behind, and nothing suggests they need another person to get it done.

## What good looks like

Weak: "ACTION REQUIRED. You have 3 overdue tasks. Complete customer outreach to stay on track."

Better:

```
Today: send your page to five people (about 20 minutes)

Why: five real reactions will show which words on your page people repeat back, and that tells us what the headline should say.

How:
1. Think of five people who own a sewing machine they never use, or who know someone who does.
2. Copy the message below and change the first line so it sounds like you.
3. Send it to each person on their own, one at a time.

Draft: "Hi Sam, I am starting small sewing classes for people who own a machine they never use. Here is the page: <link>. Is this something you or a friend would want? Honest answers help most."

When you are done: tell me how many you sent and anything anyone said. "Did some" and "Not today" are fine answers too.
```

The next day's note uses the result: "Maya, three of your five replied, and two said they were 'too scared to thread it'. That phrase is now in the page's headline draft for you to read."

## Output

```
Today: <the task, as one action> (about <n> minutes)
Why: <one sentence about their project>
How: <up to three steps>
Draft: <ready text, when one helps>
When you are done: <the one line to send back>
Serves: <this week's outcome, and the record the task builds on>
If not done: <the smaller version or the other task to offer next>
```

## Check before you hand it over

- There is one task, and no other decision is open for the founder today.
- Only a person can do it. Nothing in it is the team's or the platform's work.
- It fits in 15 to 30 minutes and can be done on a phone.
- The draft says only true things and sounds like the founder.
- Reporting back takes one line.
- You can name what the plan will do with the answer.
- No word in it reads as blame, a chore or a score.
- It costs nothing. A task that costs anything is a decision and goes out as a note with options.

## What needs the founder

The task itself and every choice inside it: who to contact, what to say, what to charge. A draft is an offer the founder can change or drop. Anything that moves money, signs or accepts terms is never tucked inside a task as a small step. It goes to the founder as its own decision.

## If a tool is missing

A founder task needs no tool beyond the note that carries it. If the page is not live yet, the task is to read the page and publish it. If nothing the task would use is ready, prepare that first and offer the task tomorrow. If you cannot store the founder's reply as a record of its own, keep it as a note on the task and say where it is.
