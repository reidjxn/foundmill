# Launch plan

## When to use this

Use this when a project is ready to be seen and nobody is waiting for it: no email list, no followers, no money for ads. It also fits a later release that is worth announcing.

A launch like this is three small launches in a row. Friends see it first, then the communities the founder already belongs to, then free directories and listings. Each ring is bigger and less forgiving than the one before, and each one finds problems that are cheaper to fix before the next.

It does not cover ongoing marketing after the launch. That is the 90-day marketing skill.

## Before you start

Read these:

1. The project's positioning notes, for the one line, the one step the page asks for and the voice.
2. The page and the product as they are today. Take the one step yourself.
3. The founder's own circles, from the records: how many people they know well enough to message, the groups they are a member of, and how many hours they have in the launch weeks.
4. Anything already announced, and what came of it.

Sort every place the launch will touch into three kinds, because they behave differently:

| Kind | What it is | Examples | Use it to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Owned | A list or page the project controls | The page, the email list, followers of the project | Receive everyone. This is where the launch ends up |
| Rented | A platform with its own rules | A social account, a group, a marketplace | Be seen quickly, and send people to what is owned |
| Borrowed | Someone else's audience | A friend's newsletter, a local podcast, a teacher's class list | Be introduced by someone already trusted |

Every post, message and listing in the plan points to one owned place: the page and its one step.

## Steps

1. **Check that it is ready.** A first version can launch when it is:
   - Simple: it does one thing, and the one line in the positioning notes says what.
   - Lovable: a person it is for would choose to use it, beyond being polite to the founder.
   - Complete: that one thing works from start to finish, with no dead buttons.

   There are two ways to get this wrong. One is polishing in private after all three are true. In that case, set a date. The other is adding more before any of the three is true. In that case, cut back to the one thing. Record which of the three hold and the evidence for each.

2. **Check the page before anyone sees it.**
   - Take the one step with a test entry and confirm where it arrives and what the person receives.
   - Open every link and view the page on a phone.
   - Paste the address into a chat and look at the preview: the title, the description and the image.
   - Confirm the page is visible to search engines and that the price and the contact details are right.
   - Give each ring its own tagged link, so visits can be told apart.
   - Know how to put the previous version back.

3. **Ring 1, friends.** Ten to thirty people the founder knows by name.
   - Each gets a short personal note from the founder, sent one at a time. The team drafts and the founder sends.
   - The note asks for two things: try it and say what was confusing, and say who else should see it.
   - The aim is to find what is broken, hear the first real words about it and gain the first followers.
   - Fix what they find. Ring 2 waits until that is done.

4. **Ring 2, communities the founder already belongs to.** A class chat, a neighborhood group, a forum they post in.
   - Read each group's rules on promotion and record the date you read them. If promotion is not allowed, the group is off the list.
   - One post per group, written for that group, by the founder as a member: what I made, why, and what I would like to know. Pasting one post into many groups reads as spam.
   - The founder answers every comment the same day. Prepare likely questions and draft answers in advance.
   - Space the groups a day or two apart, so a problem found in the first is fixed before the second.

5. **Ring 3, free directories and listings.** Places where people look for this kind of thing: launch directories for apps, map and local listings for services, marketplaces for handmade goods, newsletter directories.
   - Prepare the same kit for each: the name, the one line, a short description, three images and the tagged link.
   - Rules differ and change. As of 2026 many launch directories forbid requests for votes and expect the post to come from a real person's account. Read the current rules of each one before it goes in the plan.
   - Add one listing at a time, so the count shows which one brought people.
   - Expect a short burst and plan to catch it: the one step has to work, and the welcome message has to be ready.

6. **Plan each day that something goes out.** Say who posts what, where and at what time, and who drafts replies. Write every announcement around what a person can now do: "You can now book a trial hour." Announce only what works today.

7. **Plan the week after.**
   - A welcome message for everyone who took the step.
   - Thanks to the ring 1 friends by name, from the founder.
   - Words from early users on the page, with their permission.
   - A short review: visits by ring, steps taken, what people asked, what broke. Lessons go into "Learned so far" in the positioning notes.

8. **Keep launching.** Every real improvement is a smaller launch. Size the announcement to the change:

   | Change | Announce it with |
   | --- | --- |
   | Something new people can do | All three rings again, in order |
   | A noticeable improvement | A message to followers and one post |
   | A fix or a small addition | One line in the public log of changes |

## What good looks like

Maya tutors middle-school math and is launching a small-group "Sunday math hour" online. The one step is "Book a free trial hour". She has no list.

```
Ready: Simple yes (one hour, one grade band). Lovable yes (two of three families from her test month rebooked; booking records). Complete no: the booking page does not send a confirmation. Fix before ring 1.

Ring 1, Nov 17 to 21, 2026: 14 parents Maya knows. Note, sent by Maya one at a time:
"Hi Jo, I am starting a Sunday math hour for 6th and 7th graders, four kids at most. Would you look at the page and tell me what is unclear? And if a family comes to mind, I would love the name."

Ring 2, Nov 24 to Dec 5, 2026: the Eastside parents group (promotion allowed in the Friday thread; rules read Nov 15, 2026) and her old school's alumni group (rules not checked yet).
Ring 3, from Dec 8, 2026: the city's free tutoring listing, then one map listing. Listing terms go to Maya.

Count: visits by ring link, trial hours booked, questions asked.
Forecast: 2 or 3 trial bookings from ring 1, assuming a third of the 14 look and half of those book. Estimate until ring 1 is counted.
```

The plan says plainly that the product is not ready and what to fix first. The alumni group stays in as "not checked", so nobody posts there on a guess.

## Output

```
Launch plan: what is launching, the one step, the dates
Ready: Simple, Lovable, Complete, each yes or no with the evidence, and what to fix or cut
Page check: each item passed, failed or not checked
Ring 1, friends: how many, the draft note, what it asks, the dates
Ring 2, communities: each group, its rule on promotion and the date read, the draft post, who drafts replies
Ring 3, directories: each listing, what it needs, its terms, the date
Where everyone lands: the page, the one step, the tagged link for each ring
Count and Forecast: what is counted, and a range with its assumptions
The week after: the welcome message, the thanks, the review date
Needs the founder: every message to send, post to make and term to agree, in order
```

When the plan names a person or organization that exists as a record, use the mention format you already use.

## Check before you hand it over

- The readiness check is answered with evidence, and a "no" has a fix ahead of ring 1.
- Every item of the page check is marked passed, failed or not checked.
- The rings run in order, with time between them to fix what the last one found.
- Ring 1 notes are personal and sent one at a time. Ring 2 posts are one per group and written for it.
- Each group's rules were read, with the date, or the group is marked "not checked".
- Every post and listing leads to the one step through its own tagged link.
- Nothing asks for votes or reviews in return for anything, and no account is made up.
- No customer words, counts or deadlines appear that are not real.
- Only things that work today are announced.
- The plan describes drafts as drafts. Nothing is called sent or posted without a tool result that says so.

## What needs the founder

The founder is the sender in rings 1 and 2 and the account holder in ring 3. The team prepares the notes, posts, replies and listing kits, and the founder sends, posts and agrees to any listing terms. The launch dates, the price and any quote from a friend or customer are also the founder's to approve. Put all of it in one ordered list, so each launch day is a short run of approvals.

## If a tool is missing

- No web research: you cannot read a group's or a directory's rules. Mark each "not checked" and make reading them a task for the founder ahead of that ring.
- No tool that publishes the page: hand over the page changes as drafts and say that the page check was done on the current page only.
- No way to count visits: propose a "Where did you hear about this?" question on the one step, and count answers by hand.
