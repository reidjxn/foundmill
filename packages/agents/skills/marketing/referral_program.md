# Referral and affiliate programs

## When to use this

A referral program rewards a customer for bringing a friend. An affiliate program rewards a partner (a writer, a creator, another small business) with a commission, a share of each sale they bring. Use this to decide whether either fits the project yet, to design a small one, and to prepare its terms for the founder.

A fee to a creator for a post belongs to the creator partnerships skill.

## Before you start

Read the records for:

1. **Whether anyone is happy yet.** A referral program multiplies word of mouth that already exists. With no customers, the version that fits is "bring a friend to the waitlist", with a thank-you as the reward.
2. **The real figures.** The price, what one sale costs to deliver, refunds so far. Without them any reward is a forecast: label it an Estimate.
3. **How people already share the project,** if they do: a link, a screenshot, a word to a neighbor.
4. **What tools exist.** A way to give each person a link or code, and a record of who bought.
5. **The budget for rewards.** This is the founder's number.

Three things are fixed before design starts. Rewards, commissions and payouts are the founder's decisions: you prepare them, and the founder approves each one. You never sign terms. And nobody is rewarded for a review.

## Steps

1. **Look for sharing that needs no reward.** Some things spread through use: a thing people show to others, send to others, or use together. If the project is like that, make sharing easy first, with a link that works and a line of text ready to send. Never force it where it does not fit. Something used in private rarely spreads this way.
2. **Choose the kind of program.**

   | | Referral | Affiliate |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Who brings people | A customer | A partner, who may not be a customer |
   | Reward | Once, or a few times | A commission that continues |
   | Fits | Something people already recommend | Reaching an audience the project cannot reach alone |
   | Week-one version | "Give a friend a free first visit, and get one yourself" | One or two partners the founder knows, on written terms |

3. **Build the loop: the moment, the share, the new customer, the reward.**
   - The moment. Invite the customer right after they got what they came for: a finished job, a first result, a kind word they sent. Never at sign-up.
   - The share. One tap. A personal link, or a code that also works when spoken aloud, and a short message they can edit. It is their recommendation, in their words.
   - The new customer. The friend lands somewhere that names who sent them and what they get.
   - The reward. It arrives once the friend has paid and the refund period has passed.
4. **Choose the reward, free ones first.**

   | Reward | Cost | Fits |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | A thank-you, a mention, early access | None | Before the first sale |
   | More of the product: a free visit, an extra month | What it costs to deliver | Services and subscriptions |
   | A discount for both sides | Margin on two sales | Most small projects |
   | Cash or a gift | Cash | Later, when the figures are known |

   A reward for both sides tends to be used more than a reward for one, because the customer is giving something as well as getting it. Under about $100, state the reward in dollars. Above that, try a percentage.
5. **Size it from the figures.** Take the most the project can afford to win one customer, subtract what it already costs to win one, and what is left is the ceiling for the reward, counting both sides. Show the arithmetic, and label every forecast an Estimate. If nothing is left, propose a reward that costs no cash.
6. **For affiliates, set out the terms as decisions for the founder.**

   | Decision | To settle |
   | --- | --- |
   | Commission | A share of the sale, a flat amount, or a share that recurs. On what amount, and for how long |
   | Credit | By link or by code, how many days it lasts, and who gets credit when two partners touch one sale |
   | What counts | New customers only? How refunds and cancellations are treated |
   | Where partners may promote | Coupon sites, ads on the project's name, email. Referring yourself is out |
   | Payout | The waiting period, the minimum, the schedule, how disputes are handled |
   | Changes and ending | The notice given, and what happens to commission already earned |

7. **Require disclosure.** Anyone rewarded for a recommendation says so, clearly, beside the recommendation: "I earn a commission if you buy through this link." A friend whose link rewards them says so too. This is the US rule (the FTC's endorsement guides, as of 2026). Other countries have their own, so check before partners post.
8. **Keep reviews apart from rewards.** Invite every customer to review at the same point, whether they seemed pleased or not. Never offer anything for a review, never suggest a rating, and never draft a customer's review for them. Many review sites forbid rewards altogether (as of 2026).
9. **Guard against abuse.** Hold the reward until the friend has paid. Cap rewards per person. Take the reward back if the sale is refunded. Hold, with the evidence, anything that looks like someone referring themselves, a leaked code, or a cluster of sign-ups from one address. A pattern is a reason to look and is not proof.
10. **Measure plainly.** How many customers shared, how many friends bought, and what each new customer cost in rewards. A link or code shows who got credit. It cannot show that the sale would not have happened anyway, so say "credited to" and never "caused by".

## What good looks like

Theo walks dogs in Madison. From the records: $20 a walk, 9 customers who book about 8 walks a month each, and two of them have already sent a neighbor.

Program: "Give a neighbor a free first walk. When they book their third walk, your next one is free."

- The moment: the message Theo sends after a customer's fifth walk
- The share: a code that is the customer's dog's name, and a two-line text they can edit
- Cost per new customer: two free walks, about $40 of Theo's time (Estimate)
- The guard: one reward per new household, and it waits for the third paid walk
- The disclosure line in the text: "I get a free walk if you sign up"

Waiting for Theo: the reward itself, the cap and the wording.

It builds on sharing that was already happening, costs no cash, and leaves every decision with Theo.

Hand back:

```

Messages: <the invitation and the share text, as drafts>
How it will be measured:
Waiting for the founder: <each decision>
```

## Check before you hand it over

- The program fits the stage: cash rewards appear only once there are sales and known costs.
- The reward sits inside what the project can afford, and the arithmetic is shown.
- Every forecast is labeled Estimate, and every real figure names its record.
- The reward arrives only after a paid sale and the refund period.
- The disclosure line is written out and required.
- No reward is tied to a review or a rating.
- Terms are proposals. Nothing is signed, and no payout is described as made.
- Invitations go only to people who passed the suppression and consent checks.

## What needs the founder

The founder decides the reward, the commission, the caps and the budget. Signing partner terms and approving a payout are the founder's alone, and each payout comes to them with its evidence. Invitations to partners outside the project wait for their approval. If a program touches tax forms or contractor rules, the founder decides with the finance or legal lead.

## If a tool is missing

Without a tool that makes links or codes, ask the founder through a founder task to make one code per person, and keep the list of who holds which.

Without a payments record, the program cannot confirm sales. Say so, and count only what the founder confirms.

Without a send tool, hand the invitations over as drafts marked "not sent". A simulated payout or message is never reported as done.
