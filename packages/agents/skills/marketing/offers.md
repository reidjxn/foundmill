# Offer design

## When to use this

Use this when the page reads clearly and people still do not take the step. In that case the words are rarely the problem. The offer is: what a person gets, what they are promised, what it costs them and why they would decide today.

It also applies when someone wants a guarantee, a bonus, a time limit or a payment plan, because each of those is part of an offer and is better designed with the rest in view.

This skill fits services, classes, handmade goods, small digital products and paid newsletters. It does not set the price level. It works on everything around the price.

## Before you start

Read these:

1. The project's positioning notes: section 1 (who it is for), section 5 (proof that exists today) and section 6 (objections). The objections are your raw material.
2. The offer as it stands, wherever it is written down: the name, the price, what is included, any promise, any limit.
3. What the founder can really deliver: hours per week, units per batch, dates. A limit or a promise has to rest on one of these records.
4. "Learned so far" in the positioning notes, for offers already tried.

Keep one idea in mind. A buyer weighs the price against four things:

| Lever | The buyer's question | Raise the value by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The result | What do I end up with? | Naming the result they want, one level above the item. "A clean car without giving up your Saturday" is a result. "Car detailing" is an item |
| How sure they are | Will it work for me? | Proof from section 5, a clear method, a guarantee, saying who it is not for |
| The wait | How soon? | A first win in the first week, and a date |
| The effort and risk | What does it cost me besides money? | Doing more of it for them, fewer choices, less to learn, a safe way to try |

A request to "lower the price" usually means one of these four is weak. Fix that first. A price cut for new buyers tells them the thing is cheap, and the people it attracts tend to leave when the price returns. A lower price suits existing customers buying more, or a real seasonal sale that everyone gets.

## Steps

1. **Write the current offer in plain words.** Name, price, what they get, the promise if it fails, the limit. If a part is missing, write "none".

2. **Rate the four levers** low, medium or high, with one line of evidence each. The lowest is where to work. Change one thing per round, so you can tell what made the difference.

3. **Check the six parts.**

   | Part | Answers | Weak when |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Core | What do they get? | It lists features with no result, or leaves the scope unclear |
   | Extras | What makes the yes easier? | There are none, or they are padding |
   | Guarantee | What if it does not work? | There is none, or it promises more than the founder can stand behind |
   | A reason to decide now | Why today? | There is none, or it is not true |
   | Name | What is it called? | It is generic, or a buyer could not repeat it |
   | Price terms | What do they pay, and how? | One number with no choice in how it is paid |

4. **Core.** State the result, then the scope: what is included, what is not, what is optional. Clear scope raises trust more than a longer list does.

5. **Extras.** Each one answers a single objection from section 6.
   - It exists today, or the founder can deliver it without much time per buyer.
   - Two or three are enough for a small project.
   - Put a dollar value on an extra only when a comparable price can be shown.
   - An extra that fixes a hole in the core belongs in the core.

6. **Guarantee.** Match it to the buyer's biggest risk and to what the founder can afford.

   | Type | The promise | Fits |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Refund, no questions | Money back within a set number of days | Low-priced goods and digital products |
   | Refund with a condition | Money back if you did the work and did not get the result | Classes and programs. Two conditions at most |
   | First session out | If the first session is not right, it is free and we stop | Tutoring, coaching, services |
   | On time, or free | Delivered by the date, or the buyer owes nothing | Work with a clear delivery date |
   | No guarantee | Stated plainly, with strong proof beside it | When the founder cannot afford refunds |

   Work out what it costs if one buyer in ten uses it, and label the figure an Estimate. Write the promise in one or two specific sentences. "Satisfaction guaranteed" promises nothing. A guarantee adds to the rights buyers already have by law and never reduces them. A question about those rights goes to your lead.

7. **A reason to decide now.** Use one only when a real limit exists:
   - capacity: four slots each Saturday;
   - a batch: 24 candles in this pour;
   - a start date: the class begins Monday;
   - a first-buyers price that really rises after the stated number;
   - a bonus that really ends on the stated day.

   The buyer should be able to check the limit, and the founder has to enforce it when it is reached. Timers that reset, a "3 left" count that never moves, "people are viewing this" counters and a "last chance" that returns every week are all lies, and buyers compare notes. If no real limit exists, the offer goes out without one. Low-priced items rarely need one.

8. **Name.** Name it for the result, the method, the buyer or the timing. Test it: could a buyer text a friend "I booked the [name]. It is $60 and you get [result]"?

9. **Price terms.** The same price can be met in full, in two to four installments, as part now and the rest on delivery, monthly, or after a free first session. A plan often helps more than a lower number. Propose the terms. The founder sets them up.

10. **Leave these words out:** "game-changing", "secret", "worth $X" with no comparable, "100% guaranteed" with no conditions, and any time limit with no real end.

11. **Say how it will be checked.** Name who sees the new offer next and what will be counted. Forecast no lift. Record the result in "Learned so far".

## What good looks like

Andre details cars on weekends. His page says: "Car detailing. $60."

```
Weakest lever: how sure they are (a stranger at their home, no photos). Next: effort (do I have to be there?).

Name: The Saturday Driveway Detail
Core: Your car washed, waxed and vacuumed in your own driveway in about 90 minutes. Leave the keys and go about your day.
  Included: wash, wax, windows, vacuum, dashboard. Not included: stain removal, engine bay.
Extras: before-and-after photos by text when it is done (answers "will I see a difference?"). Free rebooking if it rains (answers "what if the weather turns?").
Guarantee: walk around the car when I finish. Anything you are not happy with I redo on the spot, or the job is free.
Reason to decide now: four cars each Saturday, because one takes 90 minutes (founder's calendar).
Price terms: $60, due when the job is done.
Cost of the guarantee: about 2 redone jobs in 20, roughly 3 hours a month. Estimate.
```

The price did not move. Four of the six parts were missing and now exist, and each one rests on something Andre can really do.

## Output

```
The offer today: name, price, what they get, promise, limit
Levers: result, sureness, wait, effort, each rated with one line of evidence
The one change this round, and why
The offer after: name; core with what is and is not included; extras with the objection each answers; guarantee; reason to decide now and the record behind it; price terms
Cost of the guarantee: an Estimate if one buyer in ten uses it
How it will be checked: who sees it next, and what is counted
Needs the founder: each decision, one line each
```

When the offer names a customer or organization that exists as a record, use the mention format you already use.

## Check before you hand it over

- The result is something the buyer said they want, in words from the positioning notes.
- Every extra answers a named objection and exists today.
- The guarantee is specific, has two conditions at most and comes with a cost Estimate.
- The limit is real, checkable and backed by a record. If there is none, the offer says nothing about time or quantity.
- Nothing is invented: no proof, no dollar values, no counts of buyers.
- A buyer could repeat the name and the result in one text message.
- Only one thing changed this round, unless the offer was being built for the first time.
- None of the words from step 10 appear.

## What needs the founder

Every part of an offer is a promise made in the founder's name, so the founder decides all of it: the price and any change to it, the guarantee, the limit, the extras that take their time, and how payment is taken. Send the offer as one decision with the cost Estimate attached. Setting up payment, and any refund, are the founder's to do.

## If a tool is missing

This skill needs no tool. If the positioning notes are missing, the objections have to come from somewhere: read what customers and followers have written, and if nothing has been collected, say that the extras and the guarantee rest on assumptions and name the five people worth hearing from first.
