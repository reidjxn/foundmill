# First 90 days of marketing

## When to use this

Use this when a project has something to show (a page, a first version, a first product) and needs a plan for finding its first followers and customers. The plan covers 90 days, costs nothing by default and fits on two pages.

It does not score or choose channels. That happens on the Channel Board. This plan takes the channels already chosen and says which moves happen in which weeks, why each should work and when to stop one. If no channels are chosen yet, propose two or three here and give the reason for each.

A single launch day has its own skill, and so does a list of ideas with no commitment behind it.

## Before you start

Read these:

1. The project's positioning notes. If there are none, write them first. A plan with no named audience is a list of chores.
2. What exists: the page, the product, the price, and every count the project has (visits, followers, orders, replies).
3. What the founder has already done to tell people, and what came of it. The plan keeps that work and builds on it.
4. The founder's limits: the hours a week they can give, and money, which is zero unless a record says otherwise.

Then settle three questions.

**How big is the problem, and how often does it come up?**

| | Comes up rarely | Comes up often |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A big problem | Winnable, but people forget the project between occasions. Be findable at the moment it happens: search, directories, word of mouth | The best place to be. Steady effort adds up |
| A small problem | The weakest spot. Put it in the decisions for the founder | Easy to try and easy to drop. Keep the price low and plan for return visits |

Name the box in one line. It limits what the plan can honestly forecast.

**Which stage holds the project back?** People move through five stages. Work on the first one that is failing.

| Stage | The question | A first thing to count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reach | Do the right strangers find out it exists? | Visits to the page, by where they came from |
| First step | Do visitors take the one step and get what was promised? | Sign-ups or orders divided by visits |
| Return | Do they come back? | Second orders, opens of the second email |
| Refer | Do they tell someone? | New people who say who sent them |
| Revenue | Does anyone pay, and is the price right? | Paid orders and the average order |

- Nobody has taken the step yet: work on Reach, once the page has passed a read-through. Sending people to a page that loses them wastes the few the project can reach.
- People visit and few take the step: First step.
- People take the step and go quiet: Return.
- People stay and growth is flat: Refer and Revenue.

A plan that spreads evenly over all five stages means this question was not answered. Below about 100 visits a rate tells you little, so say "too early to tell", plan to reach 100, and judge by reading the page and talking to people.

**How many of these people can the project reach for free?** Count the places the founder already has: friends, groups they belong to, a market stall, an email list. That number, an Estimate, is the ceiling for the first 90 days.

## Steps

1. **List candidate moves, then keep six to ten.** A move is one concrete piece of work, such as "a card in every bag that invites buyers onto the email list". No single move fixes growth. A plan works through several small ones, and the best of them keep paying after the effort stops: a page that answers a question people search for, an email list, a listing in a directory.

2. **Give every move five fields.** A move that lacks one is a hope and stays out of the plan.

   | Field | What it says |
   | --- | --- |
   | Stage | Which of the five stages it serves |
   | Why it should work | The mechanism in one sentence: who sees what, and why they act |
   | Cost | Hours for the team and hours for the founder. Money as an Estimate, zero by default |
   | Count | The one number that shows it working, and where that number comes from |
   | Stop or change if | A threshold and a date, such as "under 1 sign-up per 10 bags after three markets" |

3. **Order the moves.**
   - Repair before reach. A broken sign-up or a page with no price comes first.
   - Begin with people who have already raised a hand: friends, first followers, anyone who wanted to buy.
   - Free and lasting before paid and temporary. A paid test sits on top of something that already works, and it goes to the founder as a decision with a cost Estimate and an end date.
   - Prefer a move whose output can be used again. One good post becomes an email, a page section and the answer to a common question.
   - Plan to the founder's hours. Mark each move "team" or "needs the founder". If the founder has three hours a week, the plan uses three.

4. **Place the moves in four stretches.**

   | Weeks | Purpose | What belongs here |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | 1 to 2 | Unblock | Cheap fixes to what is broken or missing: the page, the one step, a way to count |
   | 3 to 4 | Foundation | Work that everything later reuses: the first channel started, the welcome message, five real conversations |
   | 5 to 8 | Rhythm | A weekly habit, and one small test at a time |
   | 9 to 12 | Build on what worked | More of the best move, an end to the worst, the day-90 review |

5. **Split the effort.** While nothing has clearly worked, give about 40 percent to the move with the earliest sign of life, 30 to the next candidate and 30 to small tests. Once one move clearly works, shift toward 70, 20 and 10.

6. **Forecast as a range and show the reasoning.** For example: "Forecast: 25 to 50 people on the list by week 12, from 10 market sales a week and one sign-up per four bags (an Estimate until three markets are counted)." A new project grows slowly and unevenly, so the plan draws no curve.

7. **Say what the plan leaves out.** Name three to five things the project will not do in these 90 days, each with its reason. This is what protects the founder's hours.

8. **Set the weekly review.** Each week, read the counts, hold each move against its stop rule and change one thing at most. Work out a rate only over people who have had time to act. Someone who joined yesterday has not failed to return. A move is going flat when the same effort brings fewer new people, or when the same few people keep responding. When both are true, its share of effort moves to the next candidate.

9. **Close the loop at day 90.** Record what was learned under "Learned so far" in the positioning notes and draft the next 90 days from it.

## What good looks like

A founder sells soy candles at a Saturday market, about 10 a week, and wants orders between markets. Last month the page had 40 visits and 2 orders. There is no email list.

```
Stage we work on first: Return. Market buyers like the candles and have no way to hear from the project again.
The problem: small and frequent (a candle burns down in about a month). Low price, plan for reorders.
Reach for free: about 40 market buyers a month. Estimate.

Stage | Move | Why it should work | Cost | Count | Stop or change if | Who
First step | Put the price, the delivery day and one button on the page | A buyer who scans the card cannot see what an order costs | 2 team hours | Orders divided by visits | Review at 100 visits | Team
Return | A card in every bag: "Hear first when the fall scents are ready" | The buyer already likes the product and is holding it | 1 founder hour, $12 printing (Estimate) | Sign-ups per bag | Under 1 in 10 after three markets | Founder
Return | An email every two weeks: one new scent, and what sold out | A reason to reorder arrives as the last candle runs low | 1 team hour each | Orders within 3 days of an email | No orders after three emails | Team, founder approves each

Not doing: paid ads (2 orders from 40 visits is too early to pay for more visits). Daily social posts (the founder has three hours a week).
Forecast for day 90: 25 to 50 people on the list and 3 to 6 online orders a week. Forecast.
```

## Output

Hand back the plan in this order:

```
First 90 days: the project, the start date and the end date
Summary: where the project is, the stage that holds it back, the three biggest bets (one paragraph)
The problem: size and frequency, one line
Reach for free: the number and how it was counted (Estimate)
Already done: what the founder and the team have done, and how the plan builds on it
Moves: the five fields from step 2 for each move, plus who does it
Weeks: which moves run in weeks 1 to 2, 3 to 4, 5 to 8 and 9 to 12
Not doing, and why
Forecast for day 90: a range and its reasoning, labeled Forecast
Decisions for the founder: ranked by what each one blocks
Weekly review: what is counted, and on which day
```

When the plan names a customer, person or organization that exists as a record, use the mention format you already use.

## Check before you hand it over

- One stage is named as the place to start, with the count or the observation behind it.
- Every move has all five fields. No move says "post more" or "build a community" without saying what, where and how often.
- Nothing costs money unless it has a cost Estimate and a line in the decisions for the founder.
- The founder's hours across all moves fit what the founder said they have.
- Every number that is not a real count is labeled Forecast or Estimate.
- Work the founder already did appears in the plan.
- Each item on the "not doing" list has a reason.
- Every move stays within the rules of the place where it happens. No fake accounts, no promotion where a group forbids it, no bulk messages to strangers.

## What needs the founder

The founder approves the plan before any of it runs. Four things still wait for the founder after that, each time: any spending, any message or post in the founder's name, any contact with a person outside the project, and any agreement with another business, such as a shop that would stock the product. List them under the decisions with what each one blocks, so the founder can settle them in one sitting.

## If a tool is missing

- No analytics: count by hand from what the project can see (orders, sign-ups, replies), mark page visits as unknown, and make "a way to count visits" a move in weeks 1 to 2.
- No web research: build the list of candidate moves from the positioning notes and the founder's own places, and say in your hand-back that outside channels were not checked.
