# Copy editing

## When to use this

Use this on words that already exist: a page, an email, a post, a description, or one line someone has doubts about. The aim is to improve what is there and keep its message. It also covers refreshing a published page that has gone out of date.

Writing a page from nothing and restructuring a page so it converts better have their own skills.

## Before you start

1. Read the whole piece once without changing anything.
2. From the records, note what the piece is for, who reads it, and the one action it wants.
3. Read the voice guide and the positioning notes if the project has them. Read the founder's own writing if it does not.
4. List the facts you are allowed to use: what the records hold about the product, customers, numbers and dates. An edit may sharpen a fact. It may never create one.
5. Pick the mode, and say which in one line. The mode sets how much you may change.

   | Mode | Signs | What you do |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | A. Blank or generic | Brochure language, no point of view, could be about any product | Run every pass. Rebuild sentences from the facts in the records. |
   | B. A real voice | A clear angle, recognizable word choices, rough execution | Run every pass as a restoration. Keep their argument, vocabulary and emphasis. Fix the buried point, the vague lines and the order. |
   | C. Point edit | "Fix this headline", "is this sentence OK", "tighten paragraph two" | Skip the passes. Follow step 1. |

   In modes B and C the text is evidence of the writer's choices. Their words are a voice decision ("stuff" stays "stuff"), where they spent words is a priority decision, and what they left out is a scope decision. Match those choices. Do not replace them with yours.

If the purpose or the reader is not in the records, state your assumption in the hand-back and continue.

## Steps

1. **For a point edit, change only what was named**, plus the least around it needed to read cleanly, such as a pronoun or a transition. Check the new line against step 5 and the surrounding voice so it does not stand out. Return only the changed text with one line of reason, and one alternative when the choice is close. If you notice a serious problem elsewhere, such as a false claim or a dead link, mention it in one line and leave it. Then stop.

2. **For modes A and B, make seven passes in order.** Each pass looks for one thing, because fixing everything at once misses most of it. After each pass, glance back to confirm the earlier ones still hold.

   | Pass | The question | Look for | Fix |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | 1. Clarity | Can the reader follow it on one read? | Sentences doing too much, unclear "it" and "this", unexplained terms, the point buried in qualifiers | Mark all trouble spots first, then fix. One main idea per section. Speak to the reader as "you". |
   | 2. Voice | Does it sound like one person throughout? | Casual turning formal, "we" switching to the project's name, stray technical words, humor in one place only | Read it as speech. Smooth each shift toward the voice in the records. |
   | 3. So what | Does every claim say why the reader should care? | Features with no result, achievements that do not help the reader | Add the bridge: the feature, then what it means for them. |
   | 4. Prove it | Is every claim supported? | "Trusted by many", "the best", results with no source | Attach evidence from the records. If none exists, soften the claim or mark `[NEED: ...]`. Never supply proof yourself. |
   | 5. Specifics | Is it concrete? | "Improve", "enhance", "great support", adjectives doing a noun's work | Use the real number, time or example from the records. If a line cannot be made specific, it is probably filler. Cut it. |
   | 6. Feeling | Does the reader feel the problem and the relief? | Flat description where the reader's situation should be visible | Show the "before" as a scene the reader recognizes. The feeling serves the message. Never pressure the reader, and never write urgency that is not real. |
   | 7. Risk | Is anything still stopping them at the button? | Unanswered doubts, an unclear next step, a surprise in the small print | Say what happens next. State only reassurances that are true, such as a real refund term or "cancel any time". |

3. **Run the quick checks.**

   - **Words to cut:** very, really, extremely, just, actually, basically. "In order to" becomes "to". Remove "that" where the sentence survives without it.
   - **Words to make plain:** utilize becomes use, implement becomes set up, facilitate becomes help, commence becomes start, approximately becomes about.
   - **Words to replace with the fact:** for "innovative", say what it does that the alternatives do not. For "robust", say what it withstands. For "seamless", give the real number of steps or minutes.
   - **Sentences:** one idea each. The important part first. Active voice. A verb beats a noun made from one ("decide", not "make a decision"). Usually under 25 words. Mix short and long.
   - **Paragraphs:** one topic each, two to four sentences on a page, and a first sentence that carries the point.

4. **Fix the common problems by name.**

   | Problem | Sign | Fix |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Wall of features | A list of what it does | Keep the three to five that matter most and give each its result |
   | Brochure language | Abstract nouns in a row | Write it the way the founder would say it aloud |
   | Weak opening | Starts with history or a general statement | Open with the reader's problem or the result they want |
   | Buried step | The ask arrives late or is unclear | State it early, plainly, and again at the end |
   | Mixed audiences | Tries to speak to everyone | Choose the reader the positioning notes name |

5. **Remove the patterns that read as machine-written.** A reader who spots one doubts the claims near it.

   | Pattern | Example | Fix |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Contrast reveal | "It's not X, it's Y." | State Y, with the reason |
   | List of absences | "No setup, no fees, no waiting." | Say what does happen. One true absence by the button may stay. |
   | Trailing pile-on | A claim followed by comma clauses that restate it | End at the claim. A new fact gets its own sentence. |
   | Self-answered question | "The result? Faster." | Keep the answer and delete the question |
   | Colon reveal | "The best part: it learns." | Write an ordinary sentence |
   | Stock phrases | "In today's world", "Whether you're", "Say goodbye to", "Unlock" | Cut, and open with the fact |
   | Dashes in short copy | Headlines, buttons, subject lines, posts | Use a period, comma or colon |

   Allow at most one fragment and one group of three per section. Leave alone a real question in a questions section, a genuine list of three features, and a clause that adds a new fact. Fix each pattern by going back to the fact behind it. If the fact is not in the records, write `[NEED: ...]`.

6. **Read it as three readers** when the piece matters, such as a project page, a launch email or a price page. Read once as a skeptical stranger hunting for unsupported claims, once as the customer the positioning notes describe, and once as an editor checking flow. Write down each reader's weakest spot, fix those, and read again until none of the three has a serious objection.

7. **Apply the leave-it-alone test to every change.** Revert any change that is only different and not better for a reason you can state.

8. **For a refresh of a published page, decide how deep to go.**

   | Signal | Action |
   | --- | --- |
   | Only numbers and links are old | Light refresh |
   | The message holds and the details are dated | Refresh facts, numbers and examples |
   | The project's voice has moved on | Refresh, plus the voice pass |
   | The reader or the angle has changed, or the structure no longer fits what people look for | Rewrite, using the page-writing skill |

   Then work through these:
   - **Freshness:** dates, figures, examples, and anything the project no longer offers.
   - **Accuracy:** every claim is still true, every link opens, price and features match the records.
   - **Voice:** it sounds like the project today.
   - **Proof:** add newer real evidence that did not exist when the page was written.
   - **Structure:** add a table or a short questions section where it helps a reader scan.
   - **If you can read the search results for the page's main question:** note which subtopics the top pages cover. Add what most of them cover and yours does not. Add what only one covers only when it helps your reader. Look for the useful angle none of them has. Match coverage and ignore their length.

   Keep the address, the headings other pages link to, and the voice. Show a "Last updated" date. Mark each section Keep, Revise or New in your hand-back. Suggested review rhythm: price and product pages every three months or when they change, comparison pages every three to six months, guides once a year. Never promise a position in search or a rise in visits.

## What good looks like

A founder's draft line, mode B: "Our innovative platform leverages local bakers to deliver a seamless bread experience."

Edited: "A fresh sourdough loaf on your porch every Saturday, baked that morning two streets away."

Reason: three vague words gave way to facts from the idea brief. No claim was added, and the founder's own word "porch" is back.

## Output

```
Mode: A, B or C, with one line of evidence
Edited copy: the full text, or for a point edit only the changed lines
Changes: each notable change with one line of reason, grouped by pass
Left alone on purpose: choices of the writer's that you kept
Gaps: each [NEED: ...] and what would fill it
Unsupported claims: softened or removed, with the evidence that would allow the original
For a refresh: each section marked Keep, Revise or New, and the new "Last updated" date
```

When the copy names a customer, person or organization that exists as a record, use the mention format you already use.

## Check before you hand it over

- The core message is the same as when you started.
- Every change has a reason you can state.
- No fact, number, quote, customer or deadline appears that the records do not hold.
- Every claim has support beside it, or was softened or marked as a gap.
- None of the patterns in step 5 remain. Search for them.
- In modes B and C the writer's vocabulary survives.
- A point edit changed only what was asked.
- No typos, formatting is consistent, and every link you could test opens.
- US spelling and US dates throughout.

## What needs the founder

The founder decides the voice and what the project promises. An edit that would change a price, a date, a guarantee or the meaning of a claim goes to the founder as a question, with both versions side by side. Edited copy is a draft until the founder approves it. Never replace live copy without that approval. A customer's words are never altered, and using them in public needs that customer's permission.

## If a tool is missing

- No web research: skip the search comparison in step 8, mark links as not tested, and say so.
- No way to publish: hand back the edited copy and say plainly that the live version is unchanged.
