# Writing social posts

## When to use this

Use this to write one post for one of the project's social accounts, from a topic, a pile of notes or a longer piece. Use it too when a draft exists and reads flat.

Deciding what to post and when is the social media plan skill. A page or an email has its own skill.

## Before you start

Read these first:

1. **The founder's voice.** Their own messages, their description of the idea, any posts they wrote. Note how long their sentences run, the words they reach for, and what they never do. If they never use hashtags or emoji, neither does the post.
2. **Who reads it.** The people the project is for, in the positioning notes.
3. **The one true thing the post is about,** and the record it comes from. First person ("I", "we") is used only for something the founder said happened.
4. **The network and its limits.** See step 7.
5. **What the post should lead to.** One thing: a reply, a visit to the project page, a follow.

If the notes hold a claim you cannot trace to a record, leave it out of the post and list it as a gap.

## Steps

1. **Plan before you write.** Put down two or three angles, one line each, and choose the one with the most specific fact behind it. Then choose a structure:

   | Structure | Order | Use when |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Problem, agitate, solve | The problem, why it hurts, what fixes it | The reader knows the pain |
   | Before, after, bridge | Life now, life after, how to get there | The change is easy to picture |
   | Attention, interest, desire, action | A hook, a reason to care, the benefit, the ask | You are announcing something |
   | Situation, task, action, result | What happened, what was needed, what was done, how it ended | You have a real story with an outcome |
   | Story, lesson, advice, you | A short story, what it taught, what to do, a turn to the reader | The founder learned something |
   | How-to list | Numbered steps or tips | The reader wants to do it themselves |
   | Contrarian take | A common belief, why the founder disagrees, the evidence | The founder holds the view and can back it |

2. **Start with the opening two lines.** The first states something specific in about 50 characters. The second turns it or sharpens it. Most networks cut a post after a line or two, so these decide whether anyone reads on. The skill for opening lines goes deeper.
3. **Fill the middle with the structure.** Three to five short lines per stage. Keep the real number of steps or items: if there are four, the post has four.
4. **Shape it for a phone.**
   - 20 lines of text at most, and 150 to 200 words where the network has room
   - most lines one sentence, each under about 55 characters
   - up to four short paragraphs of two or three sentences, where a thought needs them
   - a blank line between lines
   - plain words, at about a sixth-grade reading level
   - sentences that start in different ways, so they do not all begin with "I"
   - at most two lists of three

   Where the founder's own habits differ from these counts, follow the founder.
5. **End on the point.** Finish the lesson in one or two lines. One ask at most, and only a real one. Never "Agree?", "Thoughts?" or "Let that sink in".
6. **Keep it true.** Every number, customer, result and feeling comes from the records. Keep any doubt the source had: "about 30" stays "about 30". A prediction reads as the founder's opinion.
7. **Fit the network.** These limits are as of 2026. Check the current value.

   | Network | Limit | Shown before the cut |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | X | 280 characters | All of it |
   | Threads | 500 characters | The first line or two |
   | Instagram caption | 2,200 characters | About 125 |
   | LinkedIn | 3,000 characters | About 210 |
   | TikTok caption | 4,000 characters | About 150 |

   Put what matters before the cut. Use hashtags only as the founder does, and within what the network suggests. On networks that show a post with a link to fewer people, the project page link goes in the first comment or the profile, and you say so in the hand-back.
8. **Describe every image** in one sentence for people who cannot see it. If no image exists yet, say what it should show.
9. **Count the final text.** Lines, characters and words, on the exact copy you hand over.
10. **Take out the tells of machine-written posts.** "It's not X, it's Y". A list of absences ("no fees, no fuss"). A question you answer yourself. Every line written as a slogan. Emoji used as bullets. Feelings nobody had. Dashes used as pauses.
11. **Hand it over as prepared.** Unless a publishing tool is connected, the founder publishes with a tap. If the founder sends a post back three times, the angle is wrong: return to step 1.

## What good looks like

June makes ceramic mugs in Asheville. Her note in the records: "kiln broke Tuesday, lost 11 of 40 mugs. the 29 that made it have a speckle I didn't plan, the glaze got too hot. put them out Saturday anyway and three people asked if I could make more. I can't, no idea how I did it."

```
My kiln failed on Tuesday.
11 of 40 mugs cracked.

The 29 that made it came out speckled.

I did not plan the speckle.
The glaze got too hot before the kiln shut off.

On Saturday I set them out anyway.
3 people asked if I could make more.

I can't. I don't know how I did it.

So these 29 are the only ones.
They are on my page now.
```

Structure: situation, task, action, result. It is 10 lines, 71 words and 333 characters. Every number is June's, the last two lines are the ask, and "the only ones" is true.

## Output

Hand back:

```
Network:
Leads to: <the one thing>
Structure: <which, and why, in one line>
Post:
<the text, ready to paste>
Counts: <lines, characters, words>
Image: <what it shows, and the sentence describing it>
Link: <in the post, the first comment or the profile>
Two other openings: <each with a line on why>
Gaps: each [NEED: ...]
Sources: the record behind each fact
Status: prepared, not published
```

## Check before you hand it over

- The post is about one true thing, and every fact has a record.
- The opening two lines make sense alone and fit before the cut.
- The counts were made on the final text and fit the network's limit.
- It sounds like the founder: their words, their sentence length, their habits.
- "I" and "we" appear only for things the founder said happened.
- One ask at most, and no bait for comments.
- None of the tells in step 10 remain. Search for them.
- The status says "prepared" unless a tool result shows the post went out.

## What needs the founder

The founder reads the post before it is public. They publish it themselves, unless a publishing tool is connected and they have approved the post. Their approval is needed for the first post on a network, for anything that quotes them or a customer, and for any price, date or promise. A customer is named or quoted only with that customer's permission on record.

## If a tool is missing

With no publishing tool, hand the post over as a founder task with the text, the image description, the network and a suggested time, marked "prepared, not published".

If a tool queues posts, say "queued" until a tool result shows the post was sent. Never describe a post as published on the strength of a simulated result.
