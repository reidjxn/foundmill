# A month of content ideas

## When to use this

Use this when the project needs many ideas at once: a month of posts, the next few articles, or a fresh start after the ideas have run dry. It crosses the project's themes with eight kinds of piece and returns one table of specific ideas.

It does not write the pieces. If a content plan or a social media plan exists, the themes come from it.

## Before you start

Read the records for:

1. **Who the founder is and what the project does.** Their own description, a paragraph or two. The more specific it is, the more specific the ideas will be.
2. **The themes.** Three to five subjects the project returns to. Fewer than three makes a thin table, and more than five spreads it too wide. If no plan names them, propose four from the positioning notes and mark them "proposed" for the founder to confirm.
3. **The founder's voice,** so the ideas sound like theirs.
4. **What is on record to draw from.** Customer questions, things that happened, figures, photos.

## Steps

1. **Draw the grid.** Themes run down the side. Across the top go these eight kinds, always in this order:

   | Kind | What it is | It must rest on |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Actionable | A specific how-to that teaches one thing | Something the founder knows how to do |
   | Motivational | A true story of someone who did something hard in this field | A real person and a source, or the founder's own story |
   | Analytical | A breakdown of why something works the way it does | An example you can point to |
   | Contrarian | A case against common advice in the field | A view the founder holds and can back |
   | Observation | Something the founder noticed that few people mention | What they saw themselves |
   | X vs Y | A fair comparison of two tools, methods or choices | Knowledge of both |
   | Present vs future | How things are now, how the founder expects them to change, and why | A view marked as the founder's opinion |
   | Listicle | A list of tips, mistakes, lessons or resources | A real count: if there are five, the title says five |

2. **Fill every cell with a title, never a topic.** "A 3-step check before you repot" is an idea. "Repotting" is a topic. Someone should be able to start on the piece from the cell alone.
3. **Make each cell belong to its row and its column.** An idea that would sit under another theme unchanged is too general. Never repeat an idea across rows.
4. **Test each cell against the records,** and mark it:
   - Ready: it can be made from what is on record
   - Needs a fact: name the fact
   - Needs the founder: a story, a photo or an opinion only they can give

   If a cell could only be filled by making up a person, a figure or a result, change the idea until it rests on something real.
5. **Keep comparisons and opinions fair.** An X vs Y idea never runs down a named competitor. A contrarian idea argues with a practice and never with a person.
6. **Name the strongest idea.** One sentence under the table: which cell, and why it is the one to make first.
7. **Turn the table into a month.** Choose 8 to 12 cells, two or three a week. Begin with the Ready ones. Spread the kinds so the same kind never runs twice in a row, and give every theme a turn.
8. **Hand each chosen cell on** by its theme and kind ("Test nerves, Contrarian") to the skill that writes posts or articles.

## What good looks like

Ben tutors middle-school math in Columbus. His themes, from the content plan: homework without tears, test nerves, and notes for parents. One row of his table:

| Theme | Actionable | Motivational | Analytical | Contrarian | Observation | X vs Y | Present vs future | Listicle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Test nerves | The 2-minute breathing drill I use before every quiz (Ready) | How one student went from blank pages to finishing early (Needs the founder: the family's permission) | Why kids forget on a test what they knew at the kitchen table (Ready) | More practice tests will not calm an anxious kid (Ready) | The students who panic most are often the best prepared (Needs the founder: is this what Ben sees?) | Timed drills vs untimed drills in the week before a test (Ready) | Our district is moving to fewer timed tests. What I would do now (Needs a fact: the district's announcement) | 4 things to say to your kid the night before a test (Ready) |

Strongest: Test nerves, Actionable. Ben runs the drill every week, a parent can try it tonight, and it shows how he works.

Each cell is a title, each is marked, and the two that rest on a claim about a real student or a real district wait for the fact.

## Output

Hand back:

```
Themes: <three to five, each marked "from the plan" or "proposed">
Table: <themes as rows, the eight kinds as columns, one title and one mark in each cell>
Strongest idea: <the theme and kind, and one sentence on why>
The month: <8 to 12 cells in order, with the week for each>
Needs the founder: <the cells waiting on a story, a photo or an opinion>
Needs a fact: <the cells, and the fact each is missing>
```

The table itself goes back as a plain Markdown table, outside any code block, so it renders as a table.

## Check before you hand it over

- Three to five themes, eight kinds, and every cell filled.
- Every cell is a title someone could start from.
- No idea appears twice, and none would sit under another theme unchanged.
- Every cell is marked Ready, Needs a fact or Needs the founder.
- Every person, figure, quotation and result an idea depends on is real and on record, or the cell is marked as waiting for it.
- No cell runs down a named competitor or person.
- The month uses 8 to 12 cells, and the same kind never runs twice in a row.

## What needs the founder

The founder confirms any proposed themes. They supply the stories, photos and opinions that only they have, and they decide which ideas go ahead. An idea that names a customer, a student or another business needs that party's permission on record before the piece is made.

## If a tool is missing

This skill needs no tool. If the records are thin, say so, fill the table from what exists, and mark more cells "Needs the founder" so the ideas stay true.

If the place you hand back to cannot show a table, list the ideas under each theme, in the order of the eight kinds.
