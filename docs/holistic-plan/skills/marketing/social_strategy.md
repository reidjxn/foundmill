# Social media plan

## When to use this

Use this to decide where a project shows up on social networks, what it posts about, how often, and how the posts get published and reviewed. The result is a plan for the next four weeks.

Writing each post is a separate skill. So is paid advertising.

## Before you start

Read the records for:

1. **Who the project is for, and where those people already spend time.** Look for evidence: where followers said they came from, where people like them ask questions.
2. **The founder's story and voice.** Why they are building this, in their words. On every network a true story does more than any posting trick.
3. **Which accounts exist.** Plan only for the project's own accounts. Never post from a personal account, and never create an extra one.
4. **How posts get published.** Unless a publishing tool is connected, you prepare each post and the founder publishes it with a tap.
5. **How much time the founder has.** Ten minutes a day supports a different plan than an hour.
6. **What can be shown.** Photos of the work, the screen, the making, the place.

If a fact is missing, plan around it and list it as a gap in the hand-back.

## Steps

1. **Choose one network. Two at most.** A week-one project that posts well in one place does better than one that posts thinly in five.

   | Kind of network | Fits a project that | What it rewards | A cadence to start with |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | Professional (LinkedIn) | Sells to businesses | A story with a lesson, a clear opinion | 3 posts a week |
   | Short text (X, Threads, Bluesky) | Serves tech, news or a tight niche | One specific claim, replies to others | 1 post a day, plus replies |
   | Photo and video (Instagram) | Makes something people can see | Short video, swipe-through posts, the making | 3 posts a week |
   | Short video (TikTok, YouTube Shorts) | Wants strangers to find it | Unpolished video with the point in the first 2 seconds | 3 a week, more once they hold attention |
   | Groups and forums (Facebook groups, Reddit) | Is local, or serves a hobby | Useful answers. Read each group's rules first | Replies most days, few posts |

   These habits are as of 2026. Check how the network behaves now before fixing the plan.
2. **Pick two or three themes.** A theme is a subject the project returns to. Choose ones where the founder knows something others do not, where people already ask questions, and that the founder can keep feeding. A starting mix:

   | Theme | Share | Example for a plant-sitting service |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | The work, shown | 40% | Watering day at a client's apartment |
   | Something useful | 30% | How long a pothos lasts without water |
   | The people | 20% | Why the founder started, or a customer's words used with permission |
   | The ask | 10% | "Two openings left for December", and only when that is true |

3. **List what is real this week.** A post needs one true thing: something made, learned, fixed, or said by a customer. If the week has nothing to show, say so and plan a useful post. Never dress a chore up as news.
4. **Lay out the four weeks.** One row per post: date, network, theme, the true thing, format, status. Prepare a week at a time in one sitting, and leave room for a post about something that happens that day.
5. **Get several posts from one piece of work.** From any longer piece (an article, a customer conversation, a how-to) pull the parts that stand alone: a line worth repeating, a short story, one tip, one number, a moment behind the scenes. Each becomes its own post and must make sense to someone who never saw the original.
6. **Plan the replying as well as the posting.** Fifteen minutes a day, done by the founder: answer every comment, then leave a real comment on five posts from accounts the audience follows. Find 20 such accounts and list them. You draft, and the founder posts. A comment worth leaving brings a fact, an experience or a question. "Great post" brings nothing.
7. **Set the publishing path for each post.** Prepared, approved, then published. The first post on any network needs the founder's approval, and so does anything that quotes a customer or the founder. If a tool queues posts, a queued post is "queued" until a tool result shows it went out.
8. **Review each week with what can be seen.** The three posts that did best and the three that did worst, each with one line on why, marked as your reading. New followers of the project page, replies, and clicks on the tracked link matter more than likes. Take the figures from a tool if you have one. Otherwise send a founder task asking for what their app shows, and report those as the founder's figures.
9. **Change the format before the frequency.** If nothing has drawn a reply or a follower after three or four weeks, try a different format or theme. Posting more of what is not working does not help.

## What good looks like

For Rosa's plant-sitting service in Austin, with one account on a photo and video network and 20 minutes a day:

| Date | Theme | The true thing | Format | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mon, Nov 16 | The work, shown | Monday's visit: 14 plants, one with spider mites | 20-second video | Prepared, waiting for approval |
| Wed, Nov 18 | Something useful | Which plants survive two weeks alone | Swipe-through of 5 images | Prepared |
| Fri, Nov 20 | The people | Why Rosa started: she killed a friend's fern | Photo and a short story | Needs a photo from Rosa |

Replying: Rosa answers comments at 6:00 PM, then comments on five posts from Austin plant shops. Review on Sunday.

Three posts she can keep up, each built on something that happened, and the plan says who presses publish.

## Output

Hand back:

```
Network: <which, and the evidence the audience is there>
Themes: <two or three, with shares>
Four-week table: date, network, theme, the true thing, format, status
Reuse: <the longer pieces, and the posts drawn from each>
Replying: <when, and the list of accounts>
Publishing path: <the founder taps to publish, or posts are queued through a tool>
Weekly review: <the day, and where the figures come from>
Gaps: <facts, photos or approvals still needed>
```

## Check before you hand it over

- One network, two at most, each with evidence that the audience is there.
- Every planned post names one true thing from the records.
- The cadence fits the time the founder has.
- Only the project's own accounts appear.
- Posts that need approval are marked.
- A post is called published only when a tool result or the founder confirms it went out.
- Every figure in the review names its source.
- Network habits are marked "as of 2026".

## What needs the founder

The founder approves the plan, the first post on each network, and any post that quotes them or a customer. Unless a publishing tool is connected, the founder publishes each prepared post and does the replying, with your drafts. Opening an account on a new network is the founder's step, because that network's terms are theirs to agree to. Paid promotion of a post costs money: ask first, with a cost Estimate.

## If a tool is missing

With no publishing tool, each post goes to the founder as a founder task holding the text, a sentence describing the image for people who cannot see it, the network and the time. Mark it "prepared, not published".

With no analytics tool, the review uses the figures the founder reads off their app.

If a network forbids automated posting for this kind of account, the founder posts by hand there. A simulated result is never reported as a post.
