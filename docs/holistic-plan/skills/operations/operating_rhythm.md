# Running the team's week

## When to use this

Use this at the start of each round of work, when you decide what the team starts and what it holds. Use it when the same work has been blocked more than once, and once a week for the review.

A project's first seven days follow the first-week skill. This one is the rhythm for every week after that, and for any recurring work the team does unattended.

## Before you start

Read the state from records and tools. A plan made from memory of the last round repeats that round's mistakes.

1. Open tasks, what each is blocked by, and how many times it has been blocked for the same reason.
2. What waits on the founder, and how old each item is.
3. The limits you were given: agents working at once, the remaining budget, the project's daily cap on outside email, and any vacation authority.
4. What the project can really do today: take payment, publish the page, post, send email, keep code.
5. What happened outside the product since the last round: visitors, followers, replies, payments.
6. Whether the team is stopped.

## Steps

1. **Sort every action into one of two tiers.**

   | Tier | What is in it | How it runs |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | 1. Read and draft | Reading records, research, comparing, scoring, drafting, preparing a decision, updating the team's own records | On its own, every round |
   | 2. Spend, send, publish | Anything that costs money, reaches a person outside the project, goes public, deletes or hides records, or changes a live setting | Staged as a draft that waits for the founder, or run inside a limit the founder has already set for exactly that kind of action |

   The project's limits decide which tier an action is in. This skill never moves an action from tier 2 to tier 1. For the Operations lead, purchase orders, deposits, paid lab bookings and supplier agreements are the founder's every time, whatever limit is set.
2. **Run the daily round in this order.**
   1. Read the state.
   2. Finish before you start: answered decisions first, then work in review, then new work.
   3. Start tier 1 work that leads to this week's outcome and can finish with today's tools.
   4. Stage tier 2 work as drafts. Request approval only for a draft that will have a real effect once approved.
   5. Send the founder one digest, at most one decision, and their one task.
   6. Record what started, finished and stopped, and why.
3. **Check often, act rarely.** Match how often you look to how fast the thing changes: replies daily, money weekly, page visitors weekly. On a small project most checks end with "nothing to do", and that is a good result. Do not turn it into a task or a note. Forty visitors in a week is too few to read a trend from.
4. **Stay inside every cap.**
   - Agents at once, and budget: when the plan needs more, plan less. Work that cannot start is not queued.
   - Outside email: never plan more sends than the project's daily cap, and at most one message to the same person in a week unless they replied.
   - Notes: one digest a day and one open decision at a time.
   - Change: one change per round to a live page, price or message, so you can tell what caused what.
   - Near a cap, stop and say so. Never get under a cap by splitting the work into smaller pieces.
5. **Stop work that keeps getting blocked.** When a task has been blocked twice for the same cause and the team cannot fix that cause (no place for code to live, no connected channel, an approval that has waited more than three days), stop planning it. Tell the founder once, in one line, what is stuck and what would free it. Then plan the nearest work that can finish. Do not create tasks about the stuck work. A failed run of a code task is a different case: the platform retries those itself, and they are not yours to manage.
6. **Escalate these at once, whatever the tier, the limits or the vacation authority.**
   - A legal threat, a legal deadline or a demand letter.
   - A problem with a payment, an unexpected charge, a refund request or a dispute.
   - A security problem: a failed credential, a sign of a break-in, a key in the wrong place.
   - A journalist's question or a public complaint.
   - Anything about a person's safety.
   - A runway warning from the Finance lead.
   - Any action that would delete data or reach many people at once.
7. **Honor the stop.** The founder can stop the whole team at any moment. While stopped, start nothing and send nothing, and do not queue sends to go out on resume. After a resume, read the state fresh and check every staged draft again before it is used. A draft about "tomorrow's class" may be a week old. You can also stop one line of work yourself: record what you stopped and why, once.
8. **Remember between rounds.** Keep a short record of what was sent, to whom, and what the founder has already been told. Check it before any send or note, so nobody is contacted twice, nothing is explained twice, and a rerun does not repeat an action.
9. **Hold the weekly review.** Once a week, answer one question first: what real thing happened outside the product this week? Count only real results: a page live or changed, an email delivered, a post published, a payment taken, a reply or a conversation with a real person. Then list what was planned and did not finish (one line each), what you stopped, and which recurring work nobody used. Retire recurring work whose output went unused for two weeks. End with one outcome for next week. If the answer to the first question is "nothing", say so plainly and without blame, and make next week's first task the smallest real outcome the project's tools can reach.

## What good looks like

A project that sells hand-poured candles is three weeks old. The daily round finds a post draft approved yesterday, a storefront code task blocked twice with no repository connected, 31 page visitors and two replies.

The round publishes the approved post, records both replies and drafts answers for the founder to approve. It stops planning the storefront task and tells the founder once: "The online store cannot be built until there is a place for its code. Until then, orders come in by reply and a payment link." It starts a tier 1 task to prepare a price list. It sends no new decision today, because yesterday's is still open.

The weekly review reads:

```
Real this week: 1 post published, 2 replies from real people, 1 payment of $18.
Planned, not finished: storefront (stopped: no place for code).
Retired: daily visitor report (nobody used it; now weekly).
Next week's outcome: five more people see the page, and one more payment.
```

## Output

For a daily round:

```
Started: <tasks, each tier 1 and able to finish>
Staged for the founder: <tier 2 drafts that will have a real effect>
Stopped, said once: <task>: <cause, and what would free it>
Escalated: <item and why, if any>
For the founder today: <digest y/n, one decision or none, one task>
Caps: <agents, budget, sends: used of allowed>
```

For the week, use the review format in the example above.

## Check before you hand it over

- Every started task is tier 1, or tier 2 inside a limit the founder already set.
- Nothing in the plan needs more agents, budget or sends than the caps allow.
- No task appears that was blocked twice for the same unfixed cause.
- Each approval request is for work that will really happen once approved.
- Everything on the escalate list went to the founder at once.
- The weekly review counts only real results. Nothing simulated is in it.
- The founder gets at most one digest, one decision and one task today.

## What needs the founder

Every tier 2 action outside a limit they have set. Setting or changing any limit or cap. Resuming after a stop. Money, signatures, accepting terms, deleting for good and security settings, always. During a vacation, only the decision types they granted are yours to take, each with a written reason. The rest wait for them.

## If a tool is missing

If the team has no tool for a tier 2 action, the draft is the output. Hand it over marked "not sent" or "not published". Its approval request waits until a tool exists that can carry it out. If you cannot read a cap, plan as if that cap were zero and say which value was missing. If there is no record of what was sent before, send nothing to people outside until the founder has seen the draft.
