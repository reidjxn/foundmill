# The network: what to build so Foundmill spreads

Written Nov 12, 2026. A specification of the network and community side of Foundmill, written around one question: **how does Foundmill go viral?** The founder answered its questions the same day (section 14, recorded in `docs/DECISIONS.md`), and the text is revised to match: this is now the plan. Nothing in it changes a rule in FOUNDMILL.md. It gives the detail for phases 3, 4 and 5 of `docs/THESIS_REVIEW.md` §13 and adds to them; where a feature here matches a roadmap package, the package number is named.

What already exists and is built on here: a public page for every project (`/p/<slug>`) with a Follow form, a person's public profile (`/u/<handle>`) with a "looking for" answer that defaults to building alone, connections, direct and group messages, communities with moderation, push to the native app, email from Foundmill to a person, milestones, and the gateway's record of which outcomes were real. The daily streak is decided (founder, Nov 11) and counted by the server when the retention work ships; this document adds no other.

## 1. The answer in one page

**Foundmill spreads through what its founders make, and it holds people through what other people do in response.** The engine has four parts.

1. **Every real outcome is a thing worth showing, and Foundmill is the only place that can prove it is real.** Agents act through one gateway, so the platform knows that a page went live, an email went out, a stranger followed, a payment arrived. Nobody can type that into a profile. A founder's record here is made of receipts. That is what a young person with no track record wants most, and it is the answer to "why would I trust someone I met here".
2. **The loops that bring new people in are powered by a solo founder doing what is good for their own project.** Sending the page to get customers. Asking ten friends whether they would use it. Showing the card for a first dollar. None of them needs a second person on the project, so the product never has to push anyone toward a cofounder to grow. Solo is complete, and solo is also the distribution.
3. **Nobody builds alone here, even when they build on their own.** There are two ways to be with people. *Alongside*: others building their own things next to you, who see what you ship, react to it and answer your questions. *With*: someone on your project. Alongside is open to everyone, a founder building on their own included, and asks for no commitment (founder, Nov 12: nobody is excluded from the network; a solo founder may find a cofounder there, or good advice and people facing the same problems). With is one step away at every point, and always the founder's own step.
4. **Working together starts small and gets bigger only when both sides want it.** Follow, react, answer a question, share a build hour, try two weeks, become cofounders. Each step is cheap, can be undone, and leaves evidence for the next. A stranger never has to be trusted with more than the last step proved.

What gets a person hooked is one loop: **ship something real → show it in one tap → real people respond → come back to see → ship the next thing.** The reward is unpredictable (who followed, who said "I'd pay for this", who answered) and it is always attached to real work, so the habit we build is the north star itself: a real outcome every week.

Three things are deliberately absent: rankings of any kind (leaderboards were removed and stay removed), public counts on a person or a project, and any meter that reads lower for a person building alone.

## 2. Who this is for, and what moves them

**The person.** Eighteen to their late twenties. First project. Little money, so the monthly credits are a real currency to them. No business experience and nobody nearby who has done this. Lives on a phone, in group chats and short video. Has plenty of time and no map. Carries a worry about standing on their own feet that they did not come here to have named.

**What they do not have, and want.**

| They lack | So what excites them is | The network gives it as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proof that they can do things | Something real with their name on it, that a stranger can check | The record (F1), the project story (F2), cards (F3) |
| An audience | A stranger following, replying, paying. The first dollar is the most shared moment a new founder has | Signals (F8), followers, milestone cards |
| Anyone to ask | A fast, specific answer from someone one step ahead | Asks (F10), cohorts (F12) |
| People like them doing the same thing | Seeing peers ship this week, and being seen shipping | Latest (F7), cohorts (F12) |
| Money | More agent work for free | Credits for helping (F10); none for bringing people (F6, replaced Nov 19) |
| Momentum | A reason to come back tomorrow that is not guilt | Responses to their own work; the weekly rhythm (section 6) |
| Confidence about partnering | A way to try someone without betting the project | The ladder (section 4), trials and splits (F18) |

**Three kinds of member, all full members from the first minute** (THESIS_REVIEW R1).

- **The builder:** has a project, alone or not. Most of the network's content comes from their outcomes.
- **The joiner:** has no idea of their own yet, and would rather help or join. They answer asks, react, take open roles. They are the supply for every founder who wants people, and many start their own project later.
- **The outsider:** not a member. A friend asked for feedback, a follower of a project, a visitor from a shared card. Every public surface treats them as someone who may be one tap from starting.

## 3. The three questions

### 3.1 What gets them excited?

Not features. Four feelings, in the order they should arrive:

1. **"I made this."** A live page in the first session, with a story page that shows it took one person a day (F2).
2. **"Someone I don't know cares."** The first follower, the first "I'd use this" (F8). This is the moment to hand them a card (F3).
3. **"People like me are doing this, and they can see me."** The cohort they started with (F12), and their own outcomes in Latest beside everyone else's (F7).
4. **"I got paid."** The first-dollar card, verified by Foundmill, sized for a story post. Their friends are exactly the people Foundmill is for.

### 3.2 Why would they trust someone they met here?

Because on Foundmill trust does not rest on what a person says about themselves. Seven layers, each a feature:

1. **Receipts, not claims.** The record shows what the platform itself saw happen: pages live, emails sent, payments taken, questions answered, each with its date. Simulated results never count (the existing honesty rule), so the record cannot be gamed from inside the product.
2. **Seen working, not described.** Before anyone joins anything, both sides can have answered each other's asks and sat in a build hour together. How someone gives feedback tells more than a bio.
3. **Built with.** One line about a person from someone who actually shared a project or a trial with them, shown only when both agree. Nobody else can write one.
4. **A trial that cannot hurt.** A collaborator sees limited sections for two weeks. The hard gates already mean nobody, human or agent, can move money, sign, delete for good or change security without the founders. The audit chain shows who did what. Parting removes access in one step.
5. **A split that protects.** Ownership is agreed in the product with vesting in plain words, so a cofounder who leaves in month two does not keep half.
6. **Context on first contact.** A first message always arrives attached to a reason (a role, an ask, a shared cohort) and to the sender's record. No cold "hey".
7. **A platform that acts.** Report a person, a profile or a message; block in one tap; a review queue with a response target; 18 or older for everyone.

### 3.3 Why work with someone, when one person with agents can do all of it?

The product never argues this to a solo founder, and it must stay true that one person can do all of it. The honest reasons exist, and people find them by experience:

- **The work only a person can do doubles.** Agents write, build, research and send. They do not sit in a cafe with an owner, film a video, or know someone at the gym. Founder tasks are the scarce part, and a second person is a second set of them.
- **Reach doubles.** Two people have two sets of friends, two feeds, two home towns.
- **Staying power.** Most projects end because one person stops. A second person is the strongest reason not to.
- **Taste and access.** Someone who has worked behind a counter, or can draw, or speaks to the customer in their own words.
- **Credits pool.** Cofounders' included credits add up on a shared project (founder, Nov 11). It is a plain fact, stated where someone is already deciding.
- **It is more fun.**

How the product makes the case without making it: by letting people try the small steps (answering an ask is ten minutes), by showing what pairs shipped in the same feed as everything else and never larger, and by stating the facts only at the moment a founder has taken their own step toward someone.

## 4. The shape: alongside, with, and the ladder

```
Outside            a friend asked for feedback · a follower · a visitor from a card
   │  one tap: "Start your own" or "Follow"
Alongside          everyone, by default, no commitment
   ├─ follow a project or person
   ├─ send a signal ("I'd use this")
   ├─ answer an ask (ten minutes)
   ├─ start in the same cohort
   └─ share a build hour
With               the founder's own step, every time
   ├─ collaborator on a two-week trial
   └─ cofounder, with an agreed split
```

Rules of the ladder:

- **Each step is offered from the step below it, and only there.** After someone's answer helped: "Build an hour together?" After two build hours: nothing unless one of them opens it. After a good trial: the split conversation.
- **Steps are skipped freely.** Two friends go straight to cofounders through an invitation.
- **Every step can be undone**, and undoing it is written for both sides so nobody has to find the words.
- **The solo guard applies to "with", not to "alongside".** A founder whose answer is "building on my own" gets the whole alongside layer, because it is about work and asks nothing of them. They get no suggestion of a person to partner with, no open role in their feed, and no count that would be higher with two. The door to finding people stays in the same place for everyone, unbadged.

## 5. How it spreads: seven loops

Each loop names who starts it, why they start it for their own reasons, who it reaches, and what brings that person in. The figures in section 12 are working assumptions (Estimate) until measured.

| # | Loop | The founder's own reason | Reaches | The way in | Feature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | **The page is the storefront.** Every project page on the free plan carries a small "Built on Foundmill" and a link to how it was built | Getting customers | Everyone the project markets to, for as long as it lives | "How this was built: one person, six days" → "Start your own" | F2 |
| L2 | **Feedback links.** "Would you use this?" in three taps, no account | Week-one validation; the Chief of Staff already assigns "ask five people" | The founder's friends, who are the same age and in the same position | After answering: follow it, or start your own. Later: "your feedback changed this" | F4 |
| L3 | **Cards.** A milestone, a week, a chapter, as an image with an address | Pride | Friends on story posts and group chats | The card's page: the project, its Follow button, "Start yours" | F3 |
| L4 | **Invitations.** Bring a friend onto the project | Wanting that person | One person, with the strongest conversion of any loop | The invitation survives sign-up and lands them in the project | F5 |
| L5 | **Starting together.** A group starts in the same cohort | Not wanting to start alone | A friend group, a class, a Discord server | One link admits the group to the same week | F12 |
| L6 | **Open roles and people pages in search.** Public, indexable | Wanting a specific person | People searching for a project to join or a cofounder | Ask to join from the role's page | F15, F1 |
| L7 | **Bringing people, with no reward.** Replaced (founder, Nov 17; WP-14 A7 and T15 step 4): neither side gets credits. Each active founder has three named invitations | None | Multiplies L2 to L5; on the waitlist, moves the person up | A personal link on everything shared | F6 |

L1, L2 and L3 need nobody but a solo founder. That is by design: growth must not depend on the one thing the product has promised never to push.

**Attribution is part of every loop.** Every public address accepts a short `via` code naming the person and the object that brought the visitor. It survives sign-up, and it is how L8 pays out and how section 12 is measured.

## 6. How it holds: the hook

A habit forms from a prompt, an easy action, a reward that varies, and something invested that makes the next round more valuable. Foundmill's version at four speeds:

| Speed | Prompt | Action | Reward (it varies) | What they have invested |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Minutes** | The team finishes something real | "Share it": the post and card are already drafted; one tap | Over the next hours: follows, signals, replies | The record grows by one receipt |
| **Daily** | Push: "Two people said they'd use ‹project›" · the day's founder task · an ask you could answer | Open, read the short Latest, answer one thing | Someone's reply; a thank-you; credits for help that worked; the daily streak | Followers, connections, a cohort that expects you |
| **Weekly** | Friday: the week card and the weekly letter | Get one real outcome out, so the week has something on it | The week card to show; who responded; what your cohort did | Another dated line on the record |
| **Chapter** | The chapter ends | Read and share the recap | The whole chapter as one card: what you made, who responded, what you learned | A history nobody can take elsewhere |

Design rules for the hook:

- **The feed writes itself.** A first-timer does not know what to post. They never have to: every real outcome produces a drafted post from the team, and the founder taps Share or doesn't. Content supply scales with agent work, so the network is never empty of true things.
- **Latest keeps going** (founder, Nov 12). It opens on what happened since the last visit, closest people first, and then continues into the wider network for as long as someone scrolls. Every item in it is a real thing a real person did, so depth costs nothing in honesty. A quiet line marks where "since you were last here" stops.
- **Rewards come from people, never from the product pretending.** No invented activity, no sample people, no "someone viewed your profile" with nobody behind it. A founder's own numbers are of real, confirmed people, and are shown to them alone.
- **One streak.** The daily streak the founder chose stays the only one. The record shows dated lines and no totals; no ratio, no broken chain on a public page.
- **Vacation is respected.** Away mode silences people notifications and holds the place in the cohort.
- **Notifications are bundled and capped** (F20): the first follower is one push; the next ten are one push.

## 7. What to build

Twenty-one features in six groups. Each has: what it is, why it matters for spread or for holding people, the solo guard where one applies, and what it builds on.

### Group A: Proof

**F1. The record.** The person's public profile (`/u/<handle>`, package 2.3) gains a section the person cannot write by hand: what they have shipped, as the platform saw it.

- Lines: projects (with their part: founder, cofounder, collaborator), first outcomes by kind with dates ("First page live, Nov 12", "First payment, Dec 3"), recent real outcomes as dated lines, asks they answered that were marked helpful, Built with (F19). Each line appears when it happens; nothing shows as an empty slot.
- A "Verified by Foundmill" mark, with the words, on every line that came from a real gateway action. Opening it says in one sentence what was verified.
- **No counts in public** (founder, Nov 12): no follower count, no totals, no score, no level, no rank. The record is a list of dated things that happened, never a tally. No amounts unless the person chooses.
- Optional: a picture, and links whose ownership is proven (a site by a tag, GitHub by sign-in).
- **No mark on a person's page** (founder, Nov 12): "Built on Foundmill" belongs to what a person builds, never to the person.
- **Why:** it is the trust layer for everything in group E, and it is the thing a member cannot take to another product.
- **Solo guard:** a record with one name on every project reads as complete. "Built with" does not exist on the page until there is one.

**F2. The project story and the mark.** Each public project page gains two things.

- **"Built on Foundmill"**, the mark (founder, Nov 12). Small and unobtrusive, on every surface of a project on the free plan, and gone entirely on a paid plan. It is made to be met by the project's visitors more than by its founders, by living on the visitor's path and staying off the founder's:
  - **In the page footer:** one quiet line for everyone, in the smallest text the design system allows. To a signed-in Foundmill member it is plain, dim text. To a signed-out visitor it is a link to the story, and it lights softly once when the visitor reaches the end of the page.
  - **After a visitor follows:** under "Check your email to confirm", one line: "‹project› is built on Foundmill. See how it was made." A founder never meets this moment on their own page.
  - **In what the visitor receives:** the foot of the confirmation email and of the confirmation page, and a small wordmark in the corner of the link preview image.
  - **Never in the console:** the founder's preview shows the footer line once, as a visitor sees it, with "Removed on a paid plan" beside it. Nothing in Today, Needs you or the editor repeats it.
- **The story** (`/p/<slug>/story`): the project's real history as a short timeline, drafted by the team from verified events and approved by a founder. "Nov 12: the idea, in ‹name›'s words. Nov 12, 9 minutes later: this page went live. Nov 14: first follower. Nov 19: first reply from a customer." It ends with "Start your own" and, where there is one, the project's open role.
- **The log** (package 5.4): the story continues as entries, one per real outcome the founders choose to show.
- **Why:** L1. The mark asks nothing of the founder and costs their page nothing; the story is what turns a noticed mark into "I could do this". A visitor who came as a customer sees that one ordinary person made this in a week, which is the most persuasive thing Foundmill can say and it is said by someone else's work.
- **Solo guard:** "one person" is written as the achievement it is.

**F3. Cards.** An image with an address (`/m/<id>`), for anything worth showing: a milestone, a week, a chapter.

- New early milestones (package 5.3): page live, first follower, first reply from a customer, first dollar. Later ones stay.
- **What can be shown** (founder, Nov 12): only a milestone recorded from one real outside event, which today is "Page live" and "First follower". A customer or revenue milestone is counted or estimated, so it is not shown as "Verified" until it is tied to a real payment.
- Two sizes from one design: wide for link previews, tall for story posts. The project's own brand colors and logo, the fact, the date, "Verified by Foundmill".
- One tap opens the phone's share sheet.
- **Tap-to-post** (founder, Nov 12; DECISIONS): beside any drafted post, one button per network that opens that network's own share window with the text and link filled in, and "Copy text" with the image to download where a network takes no pre-filled post. Foundmill posts nothing and needs no key. A tap is recorded as a share, never as a post, and counts as a real outcome only when a visit comes back through the link. Every shared link carries `via`. The founder's test: pressing Post is the only thing done by hand. Text, link and preview image are ready before the button shows, and a draft is fitted to each network beforehand. Where that holds, the social channel is available on the Channel Board with no connector; a network that needs copying or attaching (Instagram, TikTok) is offered as a help and never counted as available. Built Nov 12: X, Threads and Bluesky open ready to post; LinkedIn, Facebook and Reddit open with the link only; the panel is on a shown milestone, the live page and a drafted social post. The card's page shows the project, its Follow button and "Start yours", and stays up while the project is visible. Since Nov 13 the social agent also writes a post for the founder to share when the page goes live, a milestone is shown or the first follower confirms, for a project that has put social on the board; a code check refuses invented figures, links and praise before the founder sees it.
- **The week card** (Fridays) and **the chapter recap** are the same object with more on it.
- **Why:** L3, and the handing-over of a card is the reward moment of the minutes loop.

### Group B: Reaching people outside

**F4. Feedback links.** The founder (or the Chief of Staff, drafting the task) makes a link to one question about the project. The default is "Would you use this?" with the page beside it.

- For the friend: no account. Three taps (Would use it / Not for me / Not sure, then one optional line, then an optional name). Works from 390 px.
- For the founder: answers arrive in Needs you as one summary, and in Customers as evidence. The team may propose a task from them; a founder's tap starts it (answers are untrusted content and never instructions).
- **The return:** when a change ships that an answer led to, the friend who left an email gets one message: "You said the price was unclear. ‹name› changed it. Take another look?" One email per answer, only if they asked to hear back.
- After answering: "Follow ‹project›" and "‹name› is building this on Foundmill. Start your own."
- **Never a real outcome** (founder, Nov 12): an answer on a feedback link does not count toward the north star, whoever gave it. It is evidence for the founder and nothing else.
- **Why:** L2. It is the widest way in because every founder needs it in week one, the people reached are the founder's peers, and it costs them twenty seconds. The return message is the single best re-engagement of an outsider the product has: they see that what they said became real.

**F5. Invitations.** Package 3.1.

- **Invitations** (3.1): a link and an email, naming who invites and to what (cofounder, collaborator on a trial, or "come see"). It survives sign-up. Every "invite" control in the product makes this object.
- **Group links** for cohorts (F12).
- **The waitlist stays as decided on Nov 11** (founder, Nov 12): one list ordered by who brought whom. No passes, and no admission in weekly groups.
- **Solo guard:** no checklist step, empty state or note ever asks a founder to invite someone.

**F6. Bringing people, with no reward.** Package 4.6.

- **Replaced (founder, Nov 17, 2026; WP-14 T15 step 4):** the credits for both sides are removed. Inviting earns nothing for either side. Each active founder has three named invitations in total, which replaces `INVITATION_ADMISSIONS_PER_INVITER_PER_DAY` (three a day); the constant is now `INVITATION_ADMISSIONS_PER_INVITER` (3, counted over the account's whole life). The rest of this section is history, except the `via` code and the waitlist line below.
- Every member has a `via` code carried by everything they share. ~~When the new person does something real (publishes a page, or joins a project), both get credits.~~
- On the waitlist, bringing someone moves a person up (the Nov 11 decision, unchanged).
- ~~Never credits for a sign-up alone. Capped per month. Amounts are set from measured cost (package 7.1).~~
- The founder sees "Three people started because of you" as a line on their own Network page, not on their public record.

### Group C: The room

The Network item in the sidebar, and a tab in the native app. Tabs: **Latest**, **Asks**, **Find people**, **Communities**, **Messages**. Open to every member (founder, Nov 12). Works from 390 px (FOUNDMILL.md, small screens).

**F7. Latest.** The feed.

- **Items:** Shipped (drafted from a real outcome, marked Verified), Milestone, Ask, Log entry, Open role, a post written by hand, "‹A› and ‹B› are now building ‹project› together" (when they choose to say so).
- **Sources, closest first:** your cohort, projects and people you follow, your communities, projects close to yours in kind, then the wider network. It keeps scrolling (founder, Nov 12).
- **No counts on a post.** Signals and follows reach the person they are for; nobody else sees a number.
- **Posting:** "Share it" on any real outcome. The draft is the team's, in the founder's voice, two sentences at most, with the card attached. Hand-written posts pass the existing screen.
- **For a member with no project:** the same feed, built from their interests, so a joiner has a reason to open it on day one.
- **Solo guard:** for "building on my own", no open roles and no suggestions of people. News of others teaming up appears as any milestone does, never larger, never with a call to action beside it.

**F8. Signals.** Four responses to a post or a project, each a word with an icon (the design system has no emoji): **I'd use this**, **I'd pay for this**, **Nice work**, **I can help**.

- "I'd use this" and "I'd pay for this" are shown to the founder alone, as real interest from confirmed people, and the sender is offered a Follow. No count is public. They show in Customers as evidence, labeled by where they came from.
- "I can help" opens a reply box.
- **Why:** a like is vanity. These are the two sentences a new founder most needs to hear from a stranger, and each one is useful to the plan.

**F9.** Not built (founder, Nov 12: a weekly public page of who shipped reads as a ranking). The number is kept so the others do not move. What it was for is carried by Latest, the week card (F3) and the cohort's week (F12).

**F10. Asks.** A small, specific request to other builders, answerable in about ten minutes.

- Kinds: **Feedback** ("What does my page say I sell?"), **Know-how** ("How do cafes choose a supplier?"), **Introduction** ("Does anyone know a gym owner?").
- The Chief of Staff offers to draft one when the plan has a question only a person can answer. The founder edits and posts.
- **Routing:** an ask is shown to people whose "Good at" tags fit, in Latest and, for those who said yes to it, in a daily push of at most one.
- **Thanks:** the asker marks an answer as helpful. That adds a dated line to the helper's record and gives the helper a small credit from Foundmill: at most one credited answer per ask, capped per helper per week, with checks against pairs trading thanks. Amounts wait for measured cost (package 7.1).
- **The step up:** after a helpful answer, each side sees "Build an hour together?" once.
- **Why:** this is where a joiner becomes useful in their first session, where strangers see each other work, and where a money-poor member can earn agent time by being generous. It is the first rung of the ladder that has any weight.
- **Solo guard:** asking for an answer is not asking for a partner; the feature never suggests one.

**F11. Starting points.** A founder who reached a milestone can publish what worked as something others start from: the kind of project, the order of the first steps, the wording that got replies. Never their customers, numbers or private plan.

- A visitor taps "Start from this" and lands in a new project with those steps in the plan.
- The publisher is told, privately, when someone starts from it.
- A public, indexable gallery.
- **Why:** remixing is the strongest spread mechanic in creative tools, and "how do I start a ‹kind› business" is what this user types into a search box. Later in the build order because it needs founders who have reached milestones.

### Group D: Alongside

**F12. Cohorts.** Everyone who starts in the same week, in groups of about thirty. (The community kind `cohort` already exists.)

- A four-week arc with one aim: a first real outcome each week.
- **The cohort's week:** one tile per member, lit when their project has shipped that week. Monday: one line on what you mean to ship. Friday: filled in from what really shipped.
- A group thread, and build hours (F13) on a fixed daily time.
- **Who is in one:** the people whose accounts opened in the same week, as the waitlist admits them. The waitlist itself is unchanged (F5).
- **Private cohorts:** one link for a friend group, a class or a Discord server to be in one cohort together, each joining it as they are admitted.
- Offered once at sign-up ("Start alongside others who begin this week? You each build your own project."), changeable later in Preferences.
- **Why:** L5, and it solves the empty room. About thirty people who start in the same week with the same four weeks ahead are a room that is alive from the first hour, however small the whole network is.
- **Solo guard:** a cohort is people building their own projects. Its week view shows projects shipping; nothing on it shows who has a partner.

**F13. Build hours.** A set time when a few people work on their own projects in the same room.

- Each says in one line what they will finish. A shared clock runs for 50 minutes. At the end each shows what they made.
- Never called a "call" and never at `/calls`: those are the founder's calls with agents.
- Version one is presence, the clock and text. Hearing and seeing each other (a small group call inside the build hour) comes later (founder, Nov 12), through a calling vendor behind the gateway.
- Scheduled for cohorts and communities, or opened by anyone for their connections.
- (The name avoids "Live sessions", which already means the agents' sessions.)
- **Why:** working next to someone is the strongest known habit-keeper for people who work alone, and an hour together is the first real evidence two people have of each other.

**F14. Communities for a purpose.** Package 4.8, on the communities that are built.

- Seeded by **place** and by **who you build for** ("for restaurants", "for students"), opened only when enough members exist, otherwise folded into a wider one.
- Post types: an update, an ask, "looking for people" (links to an open role), "show".
- A public, indexable front page for each, so "people building for restaurants in Austin" is a way in from search.
- A weekly prompt from Foundmill, labeled as from Foundmill.

### Group E: With

**F15. Open roles.** Package 4.1.

- "Needs someone who can talk to cafe owners, about five hours a week." What is offered: a trial, and what comes after if it goes well.
- Drafted by the role-writer agent when a founder asks for it; public on the project page with its own address (`/p/<slug>/roles/<id>`) and its own card.
- **Solo guard:** a role exists only because a founder opened one. No screen shows "0 open roles".

**F16. Find people.** Packages 4.2 to 4.4.

- **Browse:** people by what they are good at, what they want, hours and place; projects by kind, stage and what they need; open roles.
- **Work first, face second.** A result leads with the record and "Good at". The picture is small. This is a place to find someone to build with, and the layout should say so.
- **Five today:** for a member who said they are looking, five suggestions a day, each with one honest line of why ("You both build for restaurants; she has shipped in six weeks"). Say hi, Not now, or Follow. Five, then tomorrow. Held by an eval to stated facts only.
- **Say hi:** the introducer drafts the first message from both records; the person edits and sends. Agents never send a message between people.
- **Solo guard:** suggestions go only to someone who is looking. The matcher never orders a person lower for building alone. The tab is always there and never badged.

**F17. Messages and requests.** On the direct and group messages that are built.

- **A request from someone you are not connected to** must be attached to something: your open role, your ask, a shared cohort, a suggestion. It arrives with their record and the reason. Accept, Not now, or Block. New accounts can send few.
- **Inside a project:** a project thread, comments on notes and tasks (package 3.7).
- **Contact details:** exchanged through a button after both sides are connected, instead of typed into a message the screen would hold.
- Push and email for a first message; later ones are bundled.

**F18. From trial to cofounders.** Packages 3.3 to 3.5, 3.8 and 3.9.

- **Ask to join:** from a project page or a role, with a note and what they bring. It lands in Needs you for the founders.
- **The trial:** a two-week collaborator membership with limited sections, and a short brief both agree on first: the one thing to ship together, the hours each will give. A midpoint check-in. At the end each answers privately: continue, extend, or part.
- **Parting:** the product writes the message, removes access at once, and leaves nothing public unless both choose to show "Worked together on ‹project›".
- **Becoming cofounders:** equal standing. The split helper explains common splits and vesting with a worked example, and never recommends terms. Each person agrees with their own action; signing stays a hard gate.
- **Credits pool** from that moment, stated as a fact on the screen where they agree.
- **Why:** it makes the scariest decision a new founder faces small, reversible and already written.

**F19. Built with.** After a shared project or a completed trial, each person may write one line about working with the other.

- It shows on the other person's record only when that person accepts it.
- No stars, no numbers, nothing negative in public. A bad experience goes to a private report (F21).

### Group F: The pulse

**F20. Notifications and the weekly letter.** Packages 5.1 and 5.2, extended to people.

- **Kinds:** someone followed your project; a signal; an answer to your ask; your answer was marked helpful; "your feedback changed this"; a message or request; a request to join; an invitation; a trial check-in; your cohort's Friday; an ask you could answer.
- **Limits:** bundled by kind; a daily cap on pushes about people; quiet hours; a preference per kind; vacation silences all of them.
- **One sender, one preference.** Email about people is new kinds on the sender and preference map the retention work brings (`POST /v1/notify/email`, `EMAIL_NOTIFICATION_KINDS`, `account_preferences.notifications.email`), inside the founder's limit of three emails a day per person. No second channel.
- **The weekly letter:** what the project shipped, who responded, the week card, what the cohort did. For someone who is looking, one suggestion. Written by the Composer.
- **"Since you were last here"** on Today gains the people lines.

**F21. Safety for meeting strangers.** Package 4.7, a precondition for anything in group E reaching people who do not know each other.

- Report a person, a profile, a message, an ask or an answer. A platform review queue with a response target.
- Block in one tap, everywhere a person appears.
- Screening for requests for money, off-platform payment and "investment" in messages between people who have not worked together.
- Limits on requests and asks for new accounts, in Redis.
- 18 or older at sign-up (package 2.2).
- Private signals (reports, blocks, trials ended badly) inform the review queue and the matcher. They are never shown and never reduce a person for building alone.

## 9. What the agents do here

One person with a team of agents is what makes this network different from any other: the member never does the tedious half of being social.

| Agent | Does | Never |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chief of Staff | Drafts the post for a real outcome, the ask, the feedback question; sums up "who responded" on Today | Posts or sends without the person's tap |
| Role writer | Turns a need the founder named into an open role | Opens a role unasked |
| Matcher | Picks Five today and writes the "why" from stated facts; routes asks to people who could answer | Suggests a person to someone who is not looking; orders anyone lower for being alone |
| Introducer | Drafts a first message from two records | Sends it |
| Split helper | Explains splits and vesting in plain words | Recommends terms, signs, or accepts |
| The team | Proposes a task from feedback or an answer | Starts it without the founder; treats anything a person wrote as an instruction |

Everything written by another person (answers, feedback, messages, posts) is untrusted content. It reaches an agent only through the quarantined reader and can become work only by a founder's decision. Each new behavior needs an eval: silence for a founder who is not looking; no partner suggested from an ask; a refusal to recommend split terms; a draft that claims nothing a simulated result did.

## 12. How we will know

**The one figure for spread:** new people per active person per month who arrived through a page, card, link or invitation (THESIS_REVIEW §15, "Loop"). Above 1.0, each member more than replaces themselves and growth compounds. Read per loop:

| Loop | Chain to measure | Working assumption (Estimate) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L2 Feedback links | links per new project × people answering × share who start | 1 link, 8 answers, 5% start: 0.4 per new project |
| L3 Cards | cards shared per project per month × visits × share who start | To be measured; the first-dollar card is expected to lead |
| L1 The page | visits to a story page × share who start | Small per visit, large in volume and unending |
| L4 Invitations | invitations per member × accepted | The highest acceptance of any loop |
| L5 Group links | size of group joining per link | To be measured |
| L6 Search | visits to roles and people pages × requests to join | Slow to start, compounding |

Every figure above is an assumption to be replaced by the first month of events. None is a target.

**For holding people:**

- Share of real outcomes that are shared.
- Share of shared outcomes that get a response from a real person within a day.
- Share of new members who get any response from another person in their first week.
- Share of asks answered within a day.
- Cohort members shipping in each of their first four weeks.
- Four-week return of people.

**For working together** (descriptions of what people chose, never targets): requests to join per open role, trials started, trials that become cofounders, share of active projects with two or more people.

**The north star stays the judge:** weekly active projects with at least one real outcome. A network feature that raises visits and does not raise it is not working.

## 13. Build order

Four waves. Each feature also carries the standing definition of done from FOUNDMILL.md. The first wave spreads with no network at all, which is what a small member base needs.

**Wave 1: things that travel.** Needs only project pages, which exist.

1. Attribution and the events of section 11 (nothing else can be measured without them).
2. F2 the mark and the story.
3. F3 cards, with the four early milestones.
4. F4 feedback links, including the return message.
5. F5 invitations (package 3.1), with cofounder and collaborator as roles (3.3) so an invited friend lands somewhere real.
6. F6 bringing people (no reward since Nov 19).

**Wave 2: the room.** Needs small screens for the outer product (2.1), a home for a person (2.4), and the people layer switched on for everyone (2.5).

7. F1 the record.
8. F7 Latest with "Share it", and F8 signals.
9. F12 cohorts.
10. F20 the people notifications and the weekly letter.

**Wave 3: help, and alongside.** F21 ships first in this wave; nothing after it reaches strangers without it.

11. F21 safety.
12. F10 asks.
13. F17 message requests.
14. F13 build hours, version one.
15. F14 communities for a purpose.

**Wave 4: with.**

16. F15 open roles and F18 ask to join, the trial, parting.
17. F16 Find people: browse, then Five today and Say hi.
18. F18 cofounders, the split, pooled credits. F19 Built with.

**Later:** F11 starting points; the chapter recap; voice and video in build hours.

Reasons for the order:

- Wave 1 before the room because a room needs people and wave 1 is how they arrive.
- Invitations in wave 1 because friends who already trust each other are the safest and best-converting loop.
- Cohorts before asks because a cohort gives asks someone to be answered by.
- Finding strangers last because it is the most dependent on density and safety, and disappoints most when the room is thin.

## 15. What not to build

- **Rankings.** No leaderboard, no "top", no trending list, no levels, no points. Decided on Nov 11; and on Nov 12 the weekly "Shipped" page was declined for the same reason.
- **Anything that scores a person.** No stars, no ratings, no reputation number, no public count on a person or a project.
- **Invented activity.** No sample people in the network, no simulated responses, no "people are viewing this".
- **Agents talking to people for people.** Drafts only.
- **Money between members.** No paid help, bounties or tips. Moving money is behind a hard gate, and it changes what help means.
- **A market in ownership.** A share is something cofounders agree after working together, not something advertised.
- **Swiping on faces.** Five today leads with work.
- **Anything that pushes someone toward a partner** when they did not ask.
- **A second streak.**
