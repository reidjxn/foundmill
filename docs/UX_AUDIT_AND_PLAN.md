# UX audit and plan: approachable for a first-time founder, equally sharp for an operator

Date: Oct 20, 2026. Trigger: the first external 30-minute user test. The tester found Foundmill "exciting and interesting" and also "intimidating" and "unclear". This document is the holistic audit of why, and the plan to fix it across the whole product without losing the control-room look, the density an operator wants, or the resemblance to a management sim.

It is written against the code as it stands on `main` at 70e90ce, not against the spec. Every quoted string is real and carries its file. Twelve screenshots of the mock stack (PGlite, `MODEL_MODE=mock`, Northwind Skincare, 1440 × 900) sit in `docs/ux-audit/`. The "Reconnecting…" chip in them is a local artifact (no stream server was running), not a finding.

How to read it: §1 is the diagnosis in one page. §2 to §4 are the audit. §5 to §9 are the plan. §10 is the delivery schedule with gates. §11 is how we will know it worked. Appendices hold the complete per-slice finding lists.

## 1. Diagnosis in one page

The tester was right on both counts, and both come from the same source. The product is honest, consistent and dense, and it **assumes the reader already holds a mental model it never states**: "my team works in cycles; I press Continue to run one; what they need from me comes back as notes in the Inbox; every number is either measured or marked Estimate." A management-sim player holds that model from the first second. A student, a first-time founder, and most operators do not, and nothing on the screen gives it to them. Everything they then meet (a red "Respond · 2" before they have done anything, a status strip of "Not known yet" and "—", three near-identical mode words, a 1–20 bar with no words, an Inbox note that explains why the policy engine routed it) lands on a reader who has no frame to hang it on.

Six root causes, in order of weight:

1. **The core loop is never explained in the product.** The landing page says it once ("Press Continue, answer what comes back, and run another cycle"). Inside the app the only words are a tooltip, "Advance a business cycle", and a Day one line buried third on Home. A new founder's first contact with the signature control is its blocked, red state.
2. **Three unexplained systems arrive at once on the first two screens.** The profile asks for five 1–20 self-ratings with no anchors, target markets and risk appetite before any value is shown; Commit asks eight decisions, including Augmented / Assisted / Automated, archetype, spend cap and Autopilot, on one screen.
3. **Jargon without definitions, and several vocabularies for the same thing.** About 95 business, finance, developer and runtime terms appear in user-facing strings (3,808 hits), fewer than a third with any definition. "Needs you" is said six ways (Respond, Needs your call, Needs your go-ahead, Blocks Continue, Waiting for you, Your call). The only thing in the product called a Glossary is a rename tool.
4. **Numbers without words.** An agent shows a 1–20 skill, a 1–10 rating and 0–5 stars at once, none labeled; Form is five colored squares with the words only in aria; "Estimate" is a 10.5 px dashed chip with a native `title` as its only explanation.
5. **The design system has no guidance primitives.** No callout, explainer, coach mark, defined term, checklist or stepper; tooltips are labels; links cannot live in them; there is no account-level guidance or text-size preference. Help is a `?` key nothing advertises.
6. **Day one is an instrument panel of zeros with no next step.** Home opens on "—", "Not known yet", "0", "100 %", six "Now · Quiet" cards, an empty heatmap and an empty Journey. The useful line has no link and disappears after the first cycle.

What the plan does: it adds a **guidance layer** that is on by default and goes quiet for experts, built from a small set of new design-system primitives and one definitions source; it rewrites the first session so the first thing a founder meets is a next step, not an alarm; it puts a word beside every number and a definition on every term at first use; it collapses the "needs you" vocabulary to two; it makes the agents' prose say the business thing before the system thing; and it adds a plain-language linter so the gains hold. Structure never moves (DESIGN §2): every change below restyles, relabels, adds a line, or hides until relevant. The management-sim grammar stays and gets more legible, not less present.

What it does not do: no light theme, no mobile, no modals for waits, no simplification of the expert screens (P&L, Cap table, Plan, Live sessions, Training editor, Responsibilities, Attribution), no renaming of internal identifiers.

## 2. Who we design for, and what "approachable" means here

Four readers, one interface:

| Reader                                       | Arrives with                                                                                       | Needs from the first session                                                                                                                  | Must never feel                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Student / never thought about a business** | Curiosity, a phone's worth of app literacy, no vocabulary for runway, P&L, archetype, pull request | A reason to press the next button, every term defined where it appears, an example to look at, proof nothing can go wrong (money, signatures) | Tested, judged, locked in, charged                                                |
| **First-time founder**                       | An idea or two, some reading, fear of cost and irreversibility                                     | The loop stated once, the team's work visible, decisions with consequences in plain words, "you can change this later"                        | Behind, or that the product knows more about running a company than it tells them |
| **Serial founder / operator**                | The vocabulary, impatience, a system of their own                                                  | Density, keyboard, saved views, numbers with provenance, no tutoring in the way                                                               | Dumbed down, slowed by dismissals                                                 |
| **Management-sim player** (any of the above) | The Continue / Inbox / ratings grammar                                                             | The nod to be unmistakable and never cheesy                                                                                                   | That it is a game                                                                 |

"Approachable" therefore means: the loop is stated, every screen says what it is for, every term is defined at first use, every number carries a word, every decision carries its consequence and reversibility, every first run carries a next step, and all of that disappears behind one preference for the operator. It does not mean fewer screens, fewer numbers, or bigger buttons.

**The nod we keep** (and make more legible): the sidebar + top bar + Continue loop; the Inbox as the one place the team comes back to you; attribute ratings 1–20 in the red-amber-green ramp; profile cards with current and potential stars; Form; Training and the Coach; Health with "Down · expected back"; Hierarchy; Agent of the week; chapters; milestones in gold; leaderboards; the Channel Board. **The nod we stop leaning on**: anything a non-player needs sports knowledge to read (three unlabeled scales, Form with no key, "Natural / Accomplished / Competent / Unconvincing" with no definition, "loan", "release", "starters", "re-score").

## 3. Method

Five parallel code audits (first-run journey; shell, Home, Inbox and the cycle loop; department and deep screens; the design system's guidance affordances; language, agent prose and the spec's intent), each reading the real components and the Playwright specs that pin the real sequence, then a browser walkthrough of the mock stack at 1440 × 900 (login, profile, doors, Ideation Studio, Home, Inbox and a decision, Finance, Team, agent profile, Development Live sessions and Plan, the Channel Board, Settings › Mode, Glossary, Milestones, keyboard help). The jargon sweep reused the banned-terms scanner's own string extractor (`scripts/banned-terms.mjs` `vocabScan`), so it counts only string literals, template literals and JSX text, never identifiers.

Screens counted: about 75 distinct screens or tabs, roughly 70 within two clicks of the sidebar (37 module tabs across six modules, plus Home, Inbox, CRM and entity tabs, Milestones, the agent profile's six tabs, Settings' ten tabs, LIVE).

## 5. Principles for the fix

1. **State the loop, once, in the chrome, and again wherever it is needed.** "Your team works in cycles. Press Continue to run one. What they need from you comes back as notes in the Inbox." That sentence or its parts appear on Home's next-step strip, under Continue the first time, in the Inbox empty state, and in Continue's tooltip. Nothing else in the product makes sense without it.
2. **Say the business thing, then the system thing.** "This is yours to decide because one option spends money you can't get back", not "the options differ in cost and reversibility". The mechanism goes behind a disclosure.
3. **Define on first use, in the sentence or on the term, never only in a tooltip.** One definitions source (`packages/shared/src/terms.ts`) feeds the UI, the agents and the linter. A term the product keeps (runway, MRR, pull request, model) is defined; a term that is gratuitous (ICP, CPA, named query, exhibit, counterparty, concurrency) is replaced.
4. **Every number carries a word.** A 1–20 skill shows its band (Poor / Average / Good / Excellent), a rating shows its scale ("/10"), stars get one caption where they first appear, Form gets a key, Estimate gets a definition on hover and focus.
5. **One word for "needs you", two families at most.** _Answer_ (Respond, Needs your answer, Your answer) and _go-ahead_ (Needs your go-ahead, Give the go-ahead). "Holds Continue" for the chip.
6. **Every decision carries its consequence, its reversibility and its reason.** The DecisionBar already does the first two; the Composer schema gains `because`; the same line pattern generalizes to Stop, Vacation, Forget, Apply to all agents.
7. **Every first run carries a next step, and reassurance that it is reversible.** "You can change this later in Settings › …" under every first-run commitment.
8. **Structure never moves.** The guidance layer restyles, relabels, adds a line inside an existing region, or hides until relevant (DESIGN §2). The sidebar stays 232, the top bar 56, the list 380, the measure 640.
9. **Guided by default, quiet for experts, one switch.** `account_preferences.guidance` ("on" by default, "off" for anyone who flips it, and off automatically for accounts whose profile says Exited or Operator, which they can flip back). Off hides explainers, checklists, coach marks and term underlines; it never hides information (consequences, provenance, Estimate). Keyboard caps on buttons (Edit E, Refresh R, 1–4) are the one thing that goes the other way: they show only when guidance is off, because they are an operator's affordance and noise to a first-timer; the shortcuts work in both states and the help overlay always lists them.
10. **Never dumb down the expert screens.** P&L, Cash, Cap table, Attribution table, Plan, Live sessions, Training editor, Responsibilities, Compliance calendar keep every column. They gain an intro line and a "Simple" saved view, nothing less.
11. **Keep the nod, drop the sport.** Continue, Inbox, ratings, stars, Form, Training, Coach, Health, Hierarchy, Agent of the week, chapters, milestones, leaderboards, the Board stay. Loan, Release, starters, re-score, Natural / Accomplished / Competent / Unconvincing, and three unlabeled scales go or get words.
12. **The voice: plain, specific, short, warm, first person for agents.** Sentence case, no exclamation marks, no emoji, no "Oops", no padding to a word count (DESIGN §10, extended in §8.1).

## 8. Language system

### 8.1 Voice guide (extends DESIGN §10)

1. **Business thing, then system thing.** Before: "It needs you because the options differ in cost and reversibility." After: "This is yours to decide because one option spends money you can't get back."
2. **Define on first use, in the sentence.** Before: "Runway just crossed a warning line." After: "Runway, the months of cash left at today's spending, just dropped under 9 months."
3. **Plain verbs, no plumbing.** Before: "The exhibit below is drawn from a named query, so the figures are live rather than typed by me." After: "The chart below is live data, not my typing."
4. **Say why the recommendation is recommended.** "Recommended because it is free and reversible."
5. **Short enough to skim; no padding.** A clear 40-word note beats a 60-word one with filler. Labels get one more word when it buys meaning: "Working on now", "Next", "Your decision".
6. **Reassure where it is true.** "Nothing runs until you say go." "Nothing is deleted." "You can change this later in Settings › …" Never where it is not.
7. **Agents are colleagues, not dashboards.** First person, specific, warm, never claiming to be human, never apologizing twice.

### 8.2 The jargon table

Two tiers. **Replace** (fails the linter on sight in UI roots, option labels, task titles and template descriptions): ICP, CPA, CAC, COGS, RLS, Cedar, named query, exhibit, request_human_action, concurrency, time box, counterparty, webhook, OAuth, BYOK, unit economics, hunk, triage. **Define** (allowed when the file wraps the word in `Term`, registers it with the screen's explainer, or places it in a `hint` / `definition` / `tooltip` / `footer` prop): runway, burn, MRR, ARR, churn, P&L, cash basis, ledger, cap table, SAFE, vesting, funnel, attribution, conversion, pipeline, playbook, SKU, gross margin, payback, model, tokens, credits, pull request, branch, repository, sandbox, session, task graph, acceptance, hard gate, policy, autonomy, wage cap, Chief of Staff, cycle, chapter, Estimate, Forecast, specification, roadmap, proposal, sign-off, jurisdiction, lawful basis, archetype, fit, Form, potential. Appendix E has counts, contexts and the plain replacement for each.

## 11. How we will know it worked

**Second user test (end of Phase 3, same format: 30 minutes, external, think-aloud, one first-timer and one operator).** Tasks: create a business from an empty brief; find and answer the two sign-off notes; press Continue; say in your own words what just happened; find how many months of cash the business has and what "Estimate" means there; find an agent's skills and say what 14 means; change the business mode and say what changes. Success: both testers complete every task without help; the first-timer rates "I knew what to do next" ≥ 4/5 and "intimidating" ≤ 2/5; the operator reports no step where guidance slowed them with guidance off; time to first Continue under 8 minutes from sign-in on the mock stack.

**Product signals** (events through the outbox, no new vendor): `guidance.explainer_dismissed`, `guidance.term_opened` (by term id), `guidance.tour_completed` / `skipped`, `guidance.turned_off`; the ratio of decisions answered from the bar versus "Ask <agent>"; time from commit to first Continue; the share of first-week checklist items completed by day 7. A term opened by more than 20 % of new accounts is a candidate for in-sentence definition rather than a hover.

**Code gates**: plain-language green; jargon hits trending down per release in a `docs/PLAIN_LANGUAGE_SWEEP.md` the linter writes like `VOCAB_SWEEP.md`.
