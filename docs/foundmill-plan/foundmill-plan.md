# Vision
## Statement
Foundmill is where a person builds something of their own. One founder with a team of AI agents can build and run a real business end to end: the founder sets the direction and makes the decisions that matter, and the agents do the work across product, engineering, finding customers, money and legal, inside rules that keep money, signatures, identity and security in human hands. A founder who would rather build with someone finds them here too, and joining forces is one step, never a requirement.
## For whom
First, a first-time founder, often young, with little money and no business experience, who wants to stand on their own feet and can describe something they would like to exist. Most build alone, and the product treats that as the normal, complete case. Next, founders who choose to build together: two or three cofounders, or a founder with a collaborator who is trying the project out. Foundmill itself is the first business run this way: the team that builds Foundmill works inside Foundmill.
## Problem
More people than ever want to build something of their own, and for the first time one person can. What stops a first-timer is not effort. It is not knowing where to start, having no money to hire the skills they lack, tools that each know one corner of the business and none of the whole, and doing it with nobody beside them. AI agents can now do much of the work, but without one place that holds the plan, the history, the rules and the approvals, they are either too timid to help or too risky to trust, and what they produce stays inside a chat window.
## Why now
Models can now write and test code, publish a page, draft and send a campaign, read documents and reason over a plan well enough to do real work, and agent runtimes can run long sessions with tools. What is missing is the place around them: one plan, one team, one short list of decisions, hard gates the agents cannot cross, an honest record of what each agent did and what it cost, and other founders within reach. Foundmill is that place, and it is built and run with its own agents, so every gap in it is felt first by its own founder.
## Principles
- **Solo is complete**: one person with the agent team can do all of it. Nothing is gated on a second person, and nothing treats a project with one person as missing someone.
- **People are one step away**: finding a cofounder or a collaborator, inviting a friend, or joining someone's project works without friction at every point, and is always an offer.
- **Real outcomes**: a page that is live, an email that was sent, a payment that arrived. A simulated result is never reported as done.
- **The founder decides; agents do the work**: every decision that commits money, signs, files under the founder's identity, deletes for good or changes security waits for the founder. Everything else agents may do inside the authority the founder gave.
- **Honest by construction**: every number that is not real data is labeled Forecast or Estimate; every agent action is audited; untrusted content is data, never an instruction.
- **Plain words, and it teaches**: the product speaks plain US English, defines every term it uses, and leaves a first-time founder more capable each week.
- **Everything through the gateway**: agents reach the outside world only through one gateway that enforces policy, spending limits, rate limits and audit.
- **The cycle is a rhythm, not a stream**: read, decide, press, wait, read. Do not optimize it into continuous activity.
- **The gates are the premium**: what the team will never do without you is a selling point, stated with pride.
- **Built by its own team**: Foundmill's own engineering, product and go-to-market work runs through Foundmill. If something is painful for its own founder, it gets fixed first.
## North star
- Metric: Weekly active projects with at least one real outcome
- Definition: the number of projects in a week whose founder was active and that produced at least one real outside action or result that week (a page live, an email sent, a post published, a payment taken, a customer's reply), whatever the number of people in the project. Simulated results never count.
- Target: to be set once the first real outcomes are measured (Estimate)

# Specification
## Problem
### One person, every job
Someone starting a business has to cover product, engineering, sales, marketing, money, legal and operations. Hiring for each is out of reach; learning each alone is slow, and there is nobody to ask.

### Why today's tools fall short
- Separate tools do not share context: the plan lives in one place, the code in another, the customers in a third.
- AI assistants answer questions but rarely finish work, and when they act, the founder has no reliable way to bound what they may do.
- Most tools assume experience, a budget and a company that a first-timer does not have yet.
- Places to meet other founders are separate from the place the work happens, so a good conversation rarely turns into working together.

### What Foundmill changes
Foundmill holds the whole project in one place: its idea, its plan, its team of agents, its customers, its money and its people. Agents work from that shared plan, inside hard gates and limits, and what they make reaches the outside world: a page, a message, a way to get paid. Other founders are part of the same place.

## Customers
### First: the first-time solo founder
Someone with an idea, or the wish for one, little or no money to put in, and no experience running a business. They want to see something real exist this week and to learn as they go. They can do all of it on their own here.

The first user has one goal: a first paying customer. Development and Distribution get depth first; Finance and Legal stay competent and quiet.

### Also: founders who build together
Two or three cofounders, or a founder and a collaborator. They share one plan, one team of agents and one list of decisions, each with their own part. People are free on every plan.

### Founder zero: Foundmill itself
Foundmill's own founder runs Foundmill in Foundmill. The engineering agents develop the platform from the reidjxn/foundmill repository, the go-to-market agents run its launch, and every rough edge is felt here first.

### Who it is not for (yet)
Regulated industries that need on-premises deployment, enterprises with existing procurement and security reviews, and readers who need a language other than English.

## Value proposition
### The team you could never afford
What a founder gets here, in this order:
- Being a founder without doing every part alone: a full team of AI agents does the work, and a Chief of Staff plans it and explains it.
- Fewer ways to get it badly wrong: the house rules hold money, signatures, filings and anything that cannot be undone for the founder, whatever the team is asked to do.
- A team for company: the team is beside you at every step, and each decision arrives with a recommendation, the options and their consequences.
- Visible progress: every week ends with something that exists outside the product, and the plan moves where you can see it.

Foundmill never says it replaces a team or that a founder has no need to hire. It says what the founder can do now.

### Why a founder chooses Foundmill
- Something real, fast: a page for the project is live in the first session, and every week ends with something that exists outside the product.
- Nothing can go wrong behind your back: hard gates the agents cannot cross, spending limits, and a record of every action.
- It starts from nothing: no budget, company, network or technical account is assumed.
- It teaches: every term is defined, every step explains why, and the founder gets one thing of their own to do each day.
- People, if you want them: other founders to ask, projects to join, a cofounder to find, without leaving the place the work happens.

### What makes it hard to copy
The combination: one shared plan, a governed agent runtime with a policy gateway, connectors with real and mock versions, outcomes that reach the outside world, and a network of founders whose projects live in the same place.

## Features
### The core loop
- **Needs you**: notes written by agents with a recommendation, options, consequences and linked entities; decisions by button or free text; items that hold the work until answered.
- **Continue**: runs the team; the Chief of Staff plans the work, agents run their tasks, and the results come back as notes.
- **Today**: what the team did, what needs a decision, the founder's own task for the day, and the first-week timeline.

### What agents make real
- A public page for the project at its own address, drafted by the team and approved by the founder.
- Research from real sources, read through a quarantined reader.
- Email from a platform-provided address, with every message to someone outside waiting for the founder unless delegated.
- A waitlist form and a payment link.
- Code: branches, checks, pull requests, and a merge only the founder approves.

### Areas (labels over modules; a project can rename, merge or split them)
- **Plan**: the roadmap (Now, Next, Later) and milestones.
- **Product**: the vision, the living specification, research and sources, what changed and what is live.
- **Customers**: the Channel Board, campaigns, creative, leads, contacts and Customer Updates when a customer wins.
- **Money**: what came in, what went out and what was kept first; cash, runway (forecasts labeled), company and compliance when they become relevant.
- **Team**: every agent, with profiles, skills, training and health.
- **People**: the founders and collaborators of the project, open roles and requests to join. Hidden while the founder builds alone and has not asked for it.

### People (optional at every step)
A profile for each person, public by choice and independent of any project; a public page for each project; open roles; invitations by link; requests to join; a two-week trial as a collaborator; cofounders with equal standing; an ownership split the cofounders agree and sign themselves in the platform; connections, messages and communities with moderation.

### Guidance
Plain-language definitions on every term, explainers, first-visit tours, a Simple view of every dense table, the Chief of Staff answering "what is happening" on every page, and example projects to walk around before starting.

## Onboarding and activation
### Doors
1. **Start something**: describe an idea in your own words, or ask for ideas, and begin. The main door.
2. **Find a project or people**: for someone who would rather join or build with others. Beside the first door, never in front of it.
3. **Import**: bring what already exists (a repository, a website, documents).
4. **Example**: walk around a running example, read-only.

### From start to first real outcome
The founder names the project. The team drafts its page; the founder approves it and it is live, with a link to send to anyone. The plan (vision, specification, roadmap) is drafted meanwhile and arrives as one note to read and approve.

### Activation
A project is activated when it has its first real outcome. The target is the first session (Estimate).

## Retention loops
### Weekly
- At least one real outcome, and a letter that says what the team shipped, what it cost and who visited.
- The roadmap moves visibly: Now items close, Next items move up.
- One thing a day that only the founder can do, with a short "how".
- "Working now": while the team runs, Today shows one line for each active agent with what it is doing, and the day's digest and the weekly round-up report the effort as well as the results, from real counts only.

### Monthly
- Milestones the founder can share: page live, first follower, first conversation with a customer, first dollar.
- What the founder learned, in their own profile.

### Each chapter
The chapter report: at the end of a chapter the Chief of Staff writes one page: what we set out to do, what we did, the best moment, what we learned and what is next. Every item links to a real record, and a chapter with little in it says so.

### Vacation mode
"Take a vacation." Vacation mode is a headline feature, named on the landing page and on the Founder plan: the Chief of Staff keeps things moving within the limits the founder set, holds every hard gate, and writes a report for when the founder comes back.

## Growth
### How Foundmill spreads
Through what projects make: a project page, a milestone card, an open role and an invitation are all public links that lead back to Foundmill. A Customer Update can be shared as a card too, by the founder's own action each time. Each active founder has three named invitations to send. Inviting earns no reward for either side.

### What it never does
It never tells a solo founder they need someone, never rates a project lower for having one person, and never asks twice.

## Non-functional requirements
### Safety
- Hard gates no setting can lift: moving money, signing, accepting terms, filing under the founder's identity, deleting permanently and changing security settings always wait for the founder. A merge into a project's default branch is the founder's decision whoever is delegated.
- Every outside call goes through the gateway: policy, limits, rate limits, untrusted-content tracking and audit.
- Untrusted content is read by a quarantined reader with no tools; recipients found in it cannot be contacted without the founder.
- Data is isolated by row-level security, with a cross-tenant test for every business-scoped table.
- 18 or older to use Foundmill, confirmed once at sign-up. Between people: reporting of people, profiles and messages, blocking, and a review queue.

### Honesty
A mock connector's result is marked simulated and is never reported as an action. Invented results are never recorded for a person's own project.

### Cost
Every model call is booked to the usage ledger; credits and ceilings refuse calls before they are made when the month's credits are used up. A team that has used the month's budget rests until the date shown, and a running task always finishes first. People are free on every plan; plans differ by agent work, by the number of companies and by the features listed under Pricing.

### Screens
The outer product (public pages, sign-in, the profile, People, messages, Today and Needs you) works from 390 px. The dense console is desktop, 1280 px and wider. A native app for iOS and Android takes over on phones once it launches.

### Reliability
Every service exposes a health check. Connectors have a mock mode in which the whole golden path runs, so CI never depends on a vendor.

### Language
US English and US dates everywhere a person reads; plain-language definitions for every term; the vocabulary rules are enforced in CI.

## Data
### What Foundmill stores
The project's plan, its team (agents, versions, skills, sessions, audit), its work (tasks, workspaces, pull requests, published pages), its customers, its money (transactions, forecasts labeled as such), its legal records, its notes, and each person's profile with the visibility they chose.

### Principles
- Provenance first: every agent write records which agent, session and source it came from.
- Minimal personal data: only what a feature needs; a profile is public only where its owner said so; secrets are encrypted and never shown again.
- Portability: the founder can export the project's data.

## Integrations
### Through the gateway only
Every connector has a real implementation and a mock one, declares its tools by capability, and is held to the gateway's policy.

### Live today
- GitHub (App installation per project): read the repository, create agent branches, commit files, open pull requests, run and read the CI checks, and merge only after the founder approves.
- Domain availability from the registries.
- Anthropic on platform credits or the founder's own key; E2B sandboxes for a code task's acceptance run.

### Next
- Page publishing at a Foundmill address.
- Web research on real sources.
- Email: from Foundmill to a person, and from a project to its customers.
- Stripe for a payment link and revenue.
- One social publisher.

## Pricing
### Plans
- **Try**: the entry plan. Free while sign-up is invite-only. From the day sign-up opens, $5 a month with $5.00 of team budget, on Haiku 5.5 and GLM-5.3 Flash. One company. It has the team and everything that is not listed under another plan.
- **Founder**: tested at $15 and at $25 a month, with $10.00 of team budget a month at either price. Up to 5 companies. Opus and Sonnet without a credit pack. It adds Vacation mode, card sharing (milestone cards and Customer Updates), named agents and the phone Inbox.
- **Studio**: $50 a month where Founder is $15, and $100 where Founder is $25, with $50.00 of team budget a month. Any number of companies. It adds more agents at once.
- **Partner**: $250 a month, by application. It adds a monthly review of the project by experienced venture-backed founders and venture capital investors. Today it is a waitlist with no checkout: reviews begin once the plan opens, and no date is promised.

### On every plan
- People are free: any number of cofounders and collaborators, and no seats. A plan limits companies, never people. A deleted company does not count, and an owner over the limit keeps what they have.
- A founder can always bring their own Anthropic key. On Try, the own key is one way to reach Opus and Sonnet; a credit pack is the other.
- Everything before the first AI run is free of charge: shaping an idea, the profile, the Studio, taking charge and seeing the project. From the day sign-up opens, the first press of Continue needs Try.
- Third-party spend is billed by each provider to the founder's own accounts and never resold.

### Credit packs
A credit pack adds credits on top of the month's team budget, on any plan. Packs start at $20 and cost less per credit as they grow. On Try, a pack also unlocks Opus and Sonnet and removes the "Built on Foundmill" mark from the project's pages.

### While sign-up is invite-only
- Nobody is charged a subscription until sign-up opens, and every invited person has everything: a plan's limits and features take effect on the day sign-up opens.
- People invited while sign-up is invite-only never pay the Founder plan's monthly fee, for as long as they keep their account. They buy credit packs as anyone does.
- The Founder and Studio prices are shown only as figures marked "Indicative". When charging opens, nobody pays more than the figures they were shown, and the first price a person pays is kept for as long as they stay subscribed.
- In public, a plan reads "Free while in early access", never "Price to be announced". Apart from the promise to invited people, nothing says the product will stay free.

### Principle
Credit packs carry most of the model revenue. The subscription price is set by what the plan is worth to a founder, and it is tested, not derived from cost. Try's budget must reach the first real outcome and a first week of work, measured from the usage ledger.

## Metrics
### North star
Weekly active projects with at least one real outcome.

### Supporting metrics
- First outcome: share of new projects with a live page in the first session; median minutes to it.
- Time to the first plan and to the first approved outcome: minutes from commit, for every new project. If the median time to the first plan passes ten minutes, the "within minutes" promise on the tour is changed.
- First Continue: the share of new founders whose first press of Continue succeeds.
- Resting: the share of projects that reach Resting (the month's team budget used), and what they do next: top up, change plan, wait or leave.
- Shares: how often each Customer Update is shared as a card.
- Solo: the north star for one-person projects, read beside the rest.
- First dollar: share of projects with a first payment within 30 and 90 days.
- Return: four-week retention of people.
- Together (a description, never a target): share of active projects with two or more people; of people who looked for someone, the share who connected in their first week.
- Loop: new people per active person per month who arrived through a page, a card or an invitation.
- Cost: inference cost per active project per week against its plan's price.
- Quality: share of agent pull requests merged without a change request.

## Risks
1. **Agents that do not deliver**: an outcome that is promised and does not exist costs the founder's trust. Mitigation: a short list of outcomes that are real every time, end-to-end checks on them, and nothing pretend on screen.
2. **Cost**: free agent work on the platform's key is expensive and open to abuse. Mitigation: per-account ceilings, cheaper models for first-run work, measuring before widening sign-up.
3. **Safety between strangers**: people meeting to form partnerships can be harassed or misled. Mitigation: reporting, a review queue and an age floor before discovery opens.
4. **Pushing people together**: a solo founder who is made to feel one short leaves. Mitigation: every people feature is an offer, and the solo north star is read beside the overall one.
5. **Ownership disputes**: a split recorded in the product is serious. Mitigation: plain words, each cofounder signs with their own action, agents never sign.
6. **Scope**: the console is wide and thin. Mitigation: the first user has one goal, a first paying customer. Development and Distribution get depth first; Finance and Legal stay competent and quiet. No new console surfaces until the first real outcomes and the people basics ship.

## Architecture
### Services
- **apps/web**: the Next.js app on Vercel (foundmill.com).
- **apps/api**: Hono and tRPC on Railway; owns the product's procedures, including the people side, and runs migrations at boot.
- **apps/gateway**: the only service that calls vendors; policy, limits, rate limits, audit, and the GitHub App keys.
- **apps/orchestrator**: agent sessions (Claude Agent SDK) and Temporal workflows (cycles, vacation, training).
- **apps/composer**: writes notes; **apps/ingest**: verifies vendor webhooks; **apps/stream**: live updates to open tabs; **apps/collab**: live editing of the specification; **apps/social**: public reads.

### Packages
`packages/dugout` (the design system), packages/modules and packages/widgets (screens), packages/db (Drizzle schema, migrations, repositories, seeds), packages/agents (templates, skills, prompts), packages/connectors (real and mock connectors and their grants), packages/models (routing; model ids live only in routing.ts), packages/events (Zod event schemas and the outbox), packages/policy, packages/sandbox, packages/shared.

### Data and infrastructure
Postgres on Supabase (with row-level security), Redis for rate limits and streams, Temporal for workflows, Railway for the Node services, Vercel for the web app, GitHub Actions as the verification machine.

## Agent team and governance
### Who does what
- **Chief of Staff and Planner**: plan the work from the roadmap's Now items and the open decisions; the Chief of Staff explains and teaches.
- **Product Lead and Product Researcher**: keep the specification and roadmap true; draft the project page; propose changes as diffs.
- **Engineering Lead and the engineers**: break Now items into tasks, write the code on agent branches and open pull requests.
- **QA Engineer and Review Agent**: run the checks and review pull requests before they reach the founder.
- **Distribution Strategist and the channel engines**: the go-to-market work, on the channels that can really publish.
- **Finance Lead and Legal Lead**: the money, the entity and the filings.

### The rules every agent follows
CLAUDE.md in the repository is the authority: what Foundmill is for, the vocabulary, the never-list, the architecture rules and the definition of done. Merges into main, payments, signatures, filings under the founder's identity, permanent deletions and security changes are always the founder's.

## Engineering workflow
### How a change reaches production
1. The Engineering Lead turns a Now roadmap item into tasks with acceptance criteria and assigns them.
2. An engineer's session starts from the repository, writes the change, and the session's files are committed to the task's own branch (agent/<task>) with commit_files.
3. The QA Engineer or the Engineering Lead starts the CI checks on that branch with run_checks and reads the result with get_checks.
4. When the checks pass, the engineer opens a pull request with what changed, how it was tested and what was not verified.
5. The founder approves the merge. Merging to main deploys the web app on Vercel and the services on Railway; the api applies migrations at boot.

### Definition of done
Typecheck, lint, unit tests, a Playwright spec for any screen touched, a Storybook story for any new component, an eval for any new agent behavior, and a line in docs/PROGRESS.md; judgment calls in docs/DECISIONS.md.

### Where to read first
CLAUDE.md, docs/THESIS_REVIEW.md, docs/ARCHITECTURE.md, docs/DESIGN.md, docs/DECISIONS.md, docs/PROGRESS.md, docs/DEPLOY.md and docs/agent-pack/00-handover.md.

## Go-to-market
### Launch with Foundmill's own team
Foundmill launches using its own agents and its own loop: its project page, its milestone cards and a public build log, each approved by the founder.

### Order
1. Real outcomes proven on Foundmill itself: a page, research, an email, a payment link.
2. The waitlist opens in batches: a public page says which batch opens on which day and how many places it has. The numbers are real, and with no batch set the page shows none.
3. A personal note from the founder to each of the first hundred people admitted.
4. Foundmill's own project in public: its page, its milestone cards and its build log, run from inside Foundmill.
5. Shared cards: milestone cards and Customer Updates, each shared by its founder's own action.
6. Three named invitations for each active founder, between people who already know each other. No reward for either side.
7. The public gallery of projects, and discovery between strangers, once reporting and review are in place.
8. Paid acquisition last, only after activation and cost per active project are measured.

## Experiments
### How they run
Each experiment has one measure and a stop rule written before it starts. A test that needs analytics waits until analytics exist. The sizes and dates in the stop rules are proposals until the founder confirms them.

### The experiments
1. **Landing headline.** Today's headline against "You make the calls. Your team does the rest." and "The team you could never afford." Measure: waitlist joins per visitor. Needs analytics. Stop after four weeks or 1,000 visitors to each headline, whichever comes first, and keep today's headline unless another is clearly ahead.
2. **The public waitlist page.** Measure: organic visits and sign-ups. Visits need analytics; sign-ups are counted from the waitlist. Stop working on the page if two batches in a row bring no organic sign-up.
3. **The Founder price.** Two sets of indicative figures on the waitlist page, half of visitors seeing each: Founder $15 with Studio $50, and Founder $25 with Studio $100. Partner and credit packs are the same in both, and a visitor keeps the set they were first given. Nobody is charged. Measure: waitlist joins for each set, and at opening the share who subscribe. Stop on the day charging opens; nobody pays more than the figures they were shown.
4. **A personal note against the standard email.** Measure: the share of admitted people who reach a first real outcome. Stop at the first hundred people admitted.
5. **A card by post.** One batch receives a card from the founder by post. Measure: the same activation share, against the batch before it. Stop after that one batch.
6. **Named invitations against waitlist batches.** Measure: the share accepted, and the share who reach a first real outcome. Stop after three batches; if invited people activate no better than a batch does, nothing more is built on invitations.
7. **Resting.** Measure: what a founder does when the team rests: top up, change plan, wait or leave. Review after the first fifty projects that reach Resting; if more leave than top up or change plan, the reserve and the note are changed before anything else.
8. **"Within minutes."** Measure: the median minutes from commit to the first plan. Stop rule: past ten minutes, the line is changed to what the product delivers.
9. **Shared Customer Updates.** Measure: shares for each Customer Update; visits that come back through a card need analytics. Stop after eight weeks from the first share; if fewer than one update in ten is shared, the card stays and no more is built on it.

## Current state and known gaps
### Live in production (Nov 2026)
foundmill.com on Vercel; the services on Railway; Postgres on Supabase; migrations through 0074; the guided layer and the founder areas; GitHub live with checks and founder-only merges; models live on platform credits.

### Known gaps, in priority order
1. Nothing an agent makes reaches the internet: there is no page publishing and no deploy.
2. Production runs with mock integrations: email, payments, web research, registries and bank data are simulated.
3. Agents are granted many tools that no connector serves, so work is assigned that cannot be done.
4. Every route shows a "needs a larger window" notice below 1280 px, and sign-up is closed to a list with no waitlist.
5. A person is visible only as the owner of a committed, public business; there is no invitation, request to join, or cofounder; the people layer is switched off.
6. No message reaches a person outside the product except the sign-in link.
7. Agent outcomes are not checked end to end; the checks that exist need a live key and do not run in CI.
8. Open security items (environment default, webhook binding, a direct connector call outside the gateway, request forgery guard) must close before more connectors go live.

# Roadmap
## Now
### Agents are granted only tools that exist
Every agent template lists only tools a connector serves, the planner assigns no task that needs a tool nobody has, and channels with no publisher are shown as not available yet.
Acceptance:
- No template given to a new project holds a grant that resolves to nothing
- A tool call for an integration the project has not connected is refused as not connected, outside development and test
- The grants contract test pins both

### Security prerequisites for live connectors
Close the open security items that matter the moment email or payment webhooks go live.
Acceptance:
- The environment never defaults open
- A webhook delivery is bound to the project whose integration it belongs to
- No service other than the gateway calls a vendor
- A security review passes on each change

### A public page for every project
A public, indexable page per project at its own address, readable on a phone, with the pitch, what it is, who is building it and a follow form.
Acceptance:
- A signed-out phone opens a project's page
- A private project exposes nothing, proven by a cross-tenant test
- Each page has its own title and share image

### Agents draft and publish the page
The Product Lead drafts the page from the idea in the founder's words; the founder approves; publishing is a platform tool through the gateway, audited.
Acceptance:
- From naming the project to an approved page live in under ten minutes on live models
- An end-to-end check covers it
- Nothing is published without the founder's approval

### The first session ends on a link
The first acts become: name it, see the page drafted, approve it, send the link. The specification and roadmap are drafted meanwhile and arrive as one note.
Acceptance:
- A new project's first decision is its page
- The first-week timeline and checklist say so
- Playwright covers the new first run

### Real web research
Agents search and read real pages through the existing guards and the quarantined reader.
Acceptance:
- A research task cites real pages
- No sample page reaches a person's own project

### Email from Foundmill and from projects
One sender through the gateway, real and mock: messages from Foundmill to a person, and a platform-provided address a project can send from, with suppression and the existing approval gate.
Acceptance:
- A person receives an invitation or a "decision waiting" email
- A project sends an approved email to a customer from its own address
- Bounces and unsubscribes are handled and bound to the project

## Next

The page's form records real contacts with consent; Stripe is connected per project with hosted onboarding and an agent can create a payment link.
Acceptance:
- A stranger's signup on the page appears in Customers as a real row
- A test payment produces a Customer Update
- Anything that moves money stays the founder's

### Small screens for the outer product
Landing, sign-in, the profile, the doors, project and person pages, messages, Today and Needs you work from 390 px; the console keeps its 1280 px floor with a kind note and a way to continue later.
Acceptance:
- Playwright phone projects pass on each of those routes

Acceptance:

- Bringing someone moves a person up

### The person profile
A profile that exists without a project: what you are good at, what you want, whether you are building on your own or looking for people, with per-field visibility.
Acceptance:
- A new account with no project has a public page it controls
- "On my own" is a complete answer and asks nothing more about people
- Private fields stay private, proven by a cross-tenant test

### Invitations
An invitation by link and email that the invitee accepts or declines, and that survives sign-up.
Acceptance:
- The invitee sees who invited them and to what
- Nobody is added to a project without accepting
- A project with one person looks exactly as before

### Cofounders and collaborators
Cofounder (equal standing, any number) and collaborator (time-boxed, limited access) replace "staff"; the surface is People.
Acceptance:
- Copy says "the founders" wherever there is more than one
- People is hidden for a founder building alone who has not asked for it

### Notes for more than one person
Notes can be addressed to a person, prompts know who is in the project, and the Chief of Staff speaks to the person or the group by name.
Acceptance:
- A cofounder's notes are pitched to their own experience
- A solo founder's notes never address an absent group

Nightly sessions with tools on live models: a page published, research with real citations, an email delivered to a test inbox.
Acceptance:

Events for real outcomes, and a weekly note with the north star overall and for one-person projects.

- A weekly note reports both figures
